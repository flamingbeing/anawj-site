Hand assessment

for new hand MOs

By case

Tap the one in front of you.

Cut (finger, palm, wrist)

Check

  • Where exactly: which finger, palm side or back, radial or ulnar side, what level.
  • Resting posture: any finger straighter than the rest?
  • Colour and cap refill vs the next finger.
  • Sensation beyond the cut, each side, with 2PD. Compare with the other hand.
  • FDP and FDS of every finger near the cut (FPL for the thumb). Extensors if it's on the back. Then push against them: pain? weak?
  • Glass: X-ray.
  • Wrist cut: check median and ulnar nerves, radial and ulnar pulses, and every flexor.

Photos

  • Zoomed out + zoomed in.
  • Side view of the hand relaxed, palm up (posture).
  • Short video of each FDP and FDS test.

Escalate now if

  • Finger pale or cold.
  • Still bleeding after 10 min of pressure and elevation.
  • Wrist cut with several things cut.

Easy to miss: sensation for a finger cut needs to be tested on the finger itself, beyond the cut. Also, if the patient was gripping the knife or glass, so the tendon cut may sit further along than the skin cut.

Red, swollen finger or hand

Check

  • Kanavel's 4 signs. ↓
  • Where is the pus: nail fold, fingertip pulp, tendon sheath, web space, palm, joint?
  • Any prick, splinter, bite, manicure? Diabetic?
  • Draw the edge of the redness with a pen and write the date and time.
  • Vitals, TW, CRP, glucose. X-ray.

Photos

  • Both hands side by side, palm up (swelling only shows when compared).
  • Close-up of the finger, front and side.
  • Back of the hand.
  • After the pen marking.

Escalate now if

  • Any Kanavel sign, or the whole finger is swollen.
  • Pain out of proportion, spreading fast, dusky skin, blisters, unwell: think nec fasc.
Bite, or punch with a knuckle wound

Check

  • Fight bite: small wound over a knuckle after punching someone. The history may not mention a punch. Assume a tooth went into the joint.
  • Look at the wound with the fist clenched, the position it happened in. When the finger straightens, the tendon cut slides away and hides.
  • Extensor of that finger, against resistance.
  • X-ray: tooth, fracture.
  • Cat bites: small but deep. Check for sheath infection.

Photos

  • Back of hand, zoomed out.
  • Close-up, fingers straight and fist clenched.

Escalate now

  • Every fight bite. Most need a washout in OT.
Crush, fracture or dislocation

Check

  • Rotation: ask for a loose fist. The fingers should all point to the base of the thumb and not overlap. A finger crossing over its neighbour = rotated fracture, needs fixing. Compare with the other hand.
  • Wound over a fracture = open fracture: antibiotics and ATT as per protocol.
  • Colour and sensation beyond the injury.
  • Mallet finger: fingertip droops and can't straighten on its own.
  • X-ray the finger itself (PA + true lateral).

Photos

  • Zoomed out, fingers straight.
  • Loose fist (rotation).
  • Fingertips end-on.
  • X-rays: straight-on photo of the screen, every view.

Escalate now if

  • Dislocation not reduced, open fracture, pale finger, tense swollen hand.
Fingertip, nailbed or amputation

Check

  • How much is lost: pulp only, bone showing, through the nail?
  • Nail lifted out of its fold, nailbed cut, blood under the nail. Nailbed cut + tuft fracture = open fracture.
  • Amputation: level, clean cut or crush, time of injury, smoker, job, dominant hand.
  • Amputated part: wrap in saline-wet gauze → sealed bag → bag in ice water. Keep it off direct ice.

Photos

  • Whole hand, palm and back.
  • Stump from the front, side and end-on.
  • Amputated part, both sides, with a ruler.
  • X-ray the hand and the part.

Escalate now if

  • Amputation above the nail fold, thumb, several fingers, or a child: may be for replant.
High-pressure injection (paint, grease)

Check

  • Often a tiny hole with little pain at first, but it is an emergency.
  • What substance, when. Swelling spreading up the finger/palm, colour, sensation.
  • X-ray. Elevate. No digital block (adds pressure).

Escalate now

  • Always. Needs early debridement.
Pale, cold or blue finger

Check (vs the next finger)

  • White, flat, slow refill = no blood going in.
  • Blue, tense, very fast refill = blood not coming out.
  • Temperature. Doppler both sides if you have one. One working artery can keep a finger pink, so pink doesn't mean both are fine.

Photos

  • Whole hand in normal light, no flash (flash changes the colour).

Escalate now

  • Always. Time matters.

Sensation

Where to test: beyond the cut, each side

Each finger has two nerves, one on the radial side and one on the ulnar side. Each one supplies its own half of the finger beyond where it runs.

  • Test the fingertip pulp, radial half and ulnar half separately, for every finger beyond the wound.
  • Skin before the cut is supplied by nerve that isn't cut yet. It will always feel normal. So a normal result there doesn't tell you the nerve beyond the cut is intact.
  • Cut in the palm: one nerve there splits to supply two fingers, so it can numb the facing sides of two fingers. Test the fingers on both sides of the wound.

Light touch: always compare

  • Patient looks away. Touch the normal side, then the side at risk.
  • Ask "Does this feel the same as here?" Patients can usually feel pressure even with a cut nerve, so "can you feel this?" tends to get a yes.
  • If different: "If normal is 10, what's this?"
  • "Feels a bit funny" on the side of the cut should be treated as a nerve injury until proven otherwise.

2-point discrimination (2PD), step by step

  1. Tool: 2PD disc, or a paperclip bent into a U with the tips 5 mm apart (measure it). Both tips level and blunt.
  2. Hand resting palm up on the table, finger not moving. Show them "one" and "two" on a normal finger, then eyes closed.
  3. Place the points along the length of the finger, on one half of the pulp. Keep them off the midline: there each point lands on a different nerve, so a cut nerve can still "pass".
  4. Light pressure, just until the skin goes white.
  5. Mix one point and two points randomly, so the patient can't guess the pattern.
  6. Needs about 7 out of 10 correct to pass. If not, go wider (8, 10, 15 mm).
  7. Write the smallest distance that passed, each side, plus the same finger on the other hand.

Normal fingertip is about 5–6 mm. Above 10 mm is abnormal. Comparing sides matters more than the number: a labourer's callused hand may be 8 mm everywhere. Write: "L index: radial 12 mm, ulnar 4 mm (R index 4/4)."

Kids or can't cooperate: soak the hand in warm water for 10–30 min. Skin with a cut nerve won't wrinkle. If you can't test, write "unable to assess".

Tendons: test each one on its own

The 3D animations turn slowly; drag sideways to look from another angle.

Look first

With the hand relaxed and palm up, the fingers rest a little more bent from index to little. A finger lying straighter than its neighbours usually means its flexors are cut. This works even in a child or a patient who can't cooperate.

You can also gently bend the wrist back: the fingers should curl. A side-view photo of the resting hand is a useful record of the resting cascade.

Flexors

FDP: bends the fingertip

FDP runs all the way to the fingertip. Hold the middle phalanx so the middle joint stays straight and ask: "Bend just the tip." Test every finger near the wound.

FDS: bends the middle joint

FDS ends at the middle phalanx. Hold all the other fingers flat and straight (this stops FDP from helping) and ask them to bend the finger. It should bend at the middle joint with a floppy tip.

Index: this test is less reliable. Ask for a firm pinch with the thumb, pad to pad: a straight tip means FDS is working. Little: FDS is weak or absent in some people, so compare with the other hand.

FPL: bends the thumb tip

FPL is the only tendon that bends the thumb tip. Hold the thumb's proximal phalanx still and ask them to bend the tip, then push against it. Think of it with any cut on the palm side of the thumb, the thenar area or the wrist.

Partial cuts

  • Full movement that is painful or weak against resistance suggests a partial cut. Record it that way rather than as intact.
  • If the hand was gripping a knife or glass, the tendon was cut with the finger bent, so the tendon cut sits further along the finger than the skin cut.
  • A wound over a tendon is usually explored anyway; an accurate description of what you found is what helps most.

Extensors

Finger extensors (EDC)

Each finger has an extensor tendon on the back that straightens the knuckle (MCP). From a fist, ask them to straighten all the fingers, then press on the back of each finger's first bone in turn: each knuckle should stay straight. (Most people can't straighten the ring finger alone, so don't ask for one finger at a time.)

The small hand muscles can straighten the middle and end joints even when EDC is cut, so watch the knuckle. On the back of the hand, cross-links between tendons (juncturae) can let a neighbour lift a cut finger, but it will be weaker: compare with the other hand.

EIP and EDM

The index and little fingers each have a second extensor: EIP (index) and EDM (little). From a fist, ask them to point the index on its own (EIP) and hold it up against your finger; then the little finger on its own (EDM). The other fingers stay in the fist. Useful after cuts over the back of the wrist or the index or little metacarpal.

EPL: lifts the thumb

With the palm flat on the table, ask them to lift the thumb up off the table. You should feel the tendon tighten at the back of the thumb base. Straightening the thumb tip alone can come from the small thenar muscles, so the lift is the test.

Central slip: Elson test

The central slip straightens the middle joint (PIP). A cut on the back of the PIP can look normal on day 1; the bent-PIP deformity (boutonnière) appears days later, so test it early.

  1. Bend the middle joint 90° over the edge of the table.
  2. Rest your finger on the back of the middle phalanx and ask them to straighten the joint against you.
  3. Intact: you feel a firm push and the fingertip stays floppy. Cut: the push is weak and the fingertip goes stiff and straight.

Mallet finger: the fingertip droops and can't be straightened actively, though you can straighten it passively. X-ray for a bony fragment.

Zones (document the zone)

How to write it: "R middle: FDP bends tip fully, no pain. FDS bends with floppy tip but painful and weak compared with L. Middle lies straighter than ring at rest. Video sent."

Infection: Kanavel's signs

Flexor sheath infection: 4 signs

  1. Finger held slightly bent.
  2. Whole finger swollen like a sausage.
  3. Tender along the palm side of the finger, over the tendon.
  4. Pain when you gently straighten it (often the earliest sign).

Early on, not all 4 show. Even 1 or 2 with a swollen finger: escalate now. It can destroy the tendon within a day and usually needs OT.

Photos: part of the record

Clinical photos are part of the record, taken with the patient's consent. A close-up on its own does not show which hand, which finger, which side and how bad.

Always 2 photos:
  1. Zoomed out: whole hand and wrist, fingers spread.
  2. Zoomed in: the wound fills about half the screen, sharp.
  • Clean it first. Wipe off the blood, remove the dressing.
  • Plain background, such as a blue or green drape.
  • Good light, no shadow from your head. Flash makes wet wounds shine.
  • Phone held flat above the hand, not at an angle. Tap to focus. Move closer instead of zooming.
  • Ruler next to the wound for size.
  • Zoom into your own photo and check it's clear before sending.
  • Extras: side view of the resting hand (tendons), fist (rotation), both hands side by side (swelling), short videos of tendon tests.

Documenting and handing over

Write the findings the same way every time: it is the record, and it is what the next doctor reads. Example:

"35M, right-handed chef. Cut L index on broken glass 2 h ago, was gripping it. Lac 1.5 cm, palm side, radial side over the proximal phalanx (zone II). Pink, CRT 2 s. 2PD radial 12 mm, ulnar 4 mm (R 4/4), before LA. FDP full but painful against resistance, FDS OK. X-ray no glass. Photos + videos sent. ?Digital nerve ± partial FDP, for explore when?"
