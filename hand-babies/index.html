The 10 rules

Your senior decides from your exam and your photos. If they're wrong, the patient gets the wrong plan.

  1. Check sensation before any LA. Once ED has done a ring block, your sensation exam is gone. Ask first.
  2. Test sensation beyond the cut, never before it. Test the radial and ulnar side of the fingertip separately. Skin before the cut always feels normal. Why ↓
  3. Test every tendon on its own. "Can make a fist" is not a tendon exam. How ↓
  4. Full movement doesn't mean the tendon is OK. A tendon can be 90% cut and still move. Pain or weakness against resistance = partial cut.
  5. Look before you touch. A finger lying straighter than the others has a cut flexor. See ↓
  6. Spurting from the side of a finger = digital artery cut, so the nerve next to it is likely cut too. Press and elevate. Don't clamp blindly; you'll crush the nerve.
  7. Always send 2 photos: one zoomed out (whole hand + wrist) and one zoomed in (the wound). Wipe off the blood first. How ↓
  8. Name things properly: left/right, dominant hand, thumb / index / middle / ring / little, palm side / back, radial / ulnar side, and the level (e.g. over the middle phalanx). Not "4th finger", not "medial/lateral".
  9. Red swollen finger: check Kanavel's signs. A small wound over a knuckle after a punch is a fight bite until proven otherwise. ↓
  10. Take the rings off now, before it swells. Glass injury: X-ray for glass. Note the time of injury.

By case

Tap the one in front of you.

🔪 Cut (finger, palm, wrist)

Check

  • Where exactly: which finger, palm side or back, radial or ulnar side, what level.
  • Resting posture: any finger straighter than the rest?
  • Colour and cap refill vs the next finger.
  • Sensation beyond the cut, each side, with 2PD. Compare with the other hand.
  • FDP and FDS of every finger near the cut (FPL for the thumb). Extensors if it's on the back. Then push against them: pain? weak?
  • Glass: X-ray.
  • Wrist cut: check median and ulnar nerves, radial and ulnar pulses, and every flexor.

Photos

  • Zoomed out + zoomed in.
  • Side view of the hand relaxed, palm up (posture).
  • Short video of each FDP and FDS test.

Call now if

  • Finger pale or cold.
  • Still bleeding after 10 min of pressure and elevation.
  • Wrist cut with several things cut.

Common miss: sensation tested over the palm for a finger cut. Also: patient was gripping the knife or glass, so the tendon cut sits further down than the skin cut.

🔥 Red, swollen finger or hand

Check

  • Kanavel's 4 signs. ↓
  • Where is the pus: nail fold, fingertip pulp, tendon sheath, web space, palm, joint?
  • Any prick, splinter, bite, manicure? Diabetic?
  • Draw the edge of the redness with a pen and write the date and time.
  • Vitals, TW, CRP, glucose. X-ray.

Photos

  • Both hands side by side, palm up (swelling only shows when compared).
  • Close-up of the finger, front and side.
  • Back of the hand.
  • After the pen marking.

Call now if

  • Any Kanavel sign, or the whole finger is swollen.
  • Pain out of proportion, spreading fast, dusky skin, blisters, unwell: think nec fasc.
🦷 Bite, or punch with a knuckle wound

Check

  • Fight bite: small wound over a knuckle after punching someone. Patients often say they "hit a wall". Assume the tooth went into the joint.
  • Look at the wound with the fist clenched: that's how it happened. When the finger straightens, the tendon cut slides away and hides.
  • Extensor of that finger, against resistance.
  • X-ray: tooth, fracture.
  • Cat bites: small but deep. Check for sheath infection.

Photos

  • Back of hand, zoomed out.
  • Close-up, fingers straight and fist clenched.

Call now

  • Every fight bite. Most need a washout in OT.
🔨 Crush, fracture or dislocation

Check

  • Rotation: ask for a loose fist. The fingers should all point to the base of the thumb and not overlap. A finger crossing over its neighbour = rotated fracture, needs fixing. Compare with the other hand.
  • Wound over a fracture = open fracture: antibiotics and ATT as per protocol.
  • Colour and sensation beyond the injury.
  • Mallet finger: fingertip droops and can't straighten on its own.
  • X-ray the finger (PA + true lateral), not just "hand".

Photos

  • Zoomed out, fingers straight.
  • Loose fist (rotation).
  • Fingertips end-on.
  • X-rays: straight-on photo of the screen, every view.

Call now if

  • Dislocation not reduced, open fracture, pale finger, tense swollen hand.
💅 Fingertip, nailbed or amputation

Check

  • How much is lost: pulp only, bone showing, through the nail?
  • Nail lifted out of its fold, nailbed cut, blood under the nail. Nailbed cut + tuft fracture = open fracture.
  • Amputation: level, clean cut or crush, time of injury, smoker, job, dominant hand.
  • Amputated part: wrap in saline-wet gauze → sealed bag → bag in ice water. Never directly on ice.

Photos

  • Whole hand, palm and back.
  • Stump from the front, side and end-on.
  • Amputated part, both sides, with a ruler.
  • X-ray the hand and the part.

Call now if

  • Amputation above the nail fold, thumb, several fingers, or a child: may be for replant.
💉 High-pressure injection (paint, grease)

Check

  • Tiny hole, looks harmless, often not much pain at first. It's an emergency.
  • What substance, when. Swelling spreading up the finger/palm, colour, sensation.
  • X-ray. Elevate. No digital block (adds pressure).

Call now

  • Always. Needs early debridement.
🩸 Pale, cold or blue finger

Check (vs the next finger)

  • White, flat, slow refill = no blood going in.
  • Blue, tense, very fast refill = blood not coming out.
  • Temperature. Doppler both sides if you have one. One working artery can keep a finger pink, so pink doesn't mean both are fine.

Photos

  • Whole hand in normal light, no flash (flash changes the colour).

Call now

  • Always. Time matters.

Sensation

Where to test: beyond the cut, each side

Each finger has two nerves, one on the radial side and one on the ulnar side. Each one supplies its own half of the finger beyond where it runs.

  • Test the fingertip pulp, radial half and ulnar half separately, for every finger beyond the wound.
  • Skin before the cut is supplied by nerve that isn't cut yet. It will always feel normal. Testing there and writing "sensation intact" is wrong.
  • Cut in the palm: one nerve there splits to supply two fingers, so it can numb the facing sides of two fingers. Test the fingers on both sides of the wound.

Light touch: always compare

  • Patient looks away. Touch the normal side, then the side at risk.
  • Ask "Same or different?", not "can you feel this?" (they'll say yes even with a cut nerve, because they feel the pressure).
  • If different: "If normal is 10, what's this?"
  • "Feels a bit funny" on the side of the cut = nerve cut until proven otherwise.

2-point discrimination (2PD), done properly

  1. Tool: 2PD disc, or a paperclip bent into a U with the tips 5 mm apart (measure it). Both tips level and blunt.
  2. Hand resting palm up on the table, finger not moving. Show them "one" and "two" on a normal finger, then eyes closed.
  3. Points along the length of the finger, on one half of the pulp. Never across the middle: each point then lands on a different nerve, and even a cut nerve "passes".
  4. Light pressure, just until the skin goes white. Don't dig in.
  5. Mix one point and two points randomly. Don't always start with two.
  6. Needs about 7 out of 10 correct to pass. If not, go wider (8, 10, 15 mm).
  7. Write the smallest distance that passed, each side, plus the same finger on the other hand.

Normal fingertip is about 5–6 mm. Above 10 mm is abnormal. But compare, don't just quote numbers: a labourer's callused hand may be 8 mm everywhere. Write: "L index: radial 12 mm, ulnar 4 mm (R index 4/4)."

Kids or can't cooperate: soak the hand in warm water for 10–30 min. Skin with a cut nerve won't wrinkle. If you really can't test, write "unable to assess", not "intact".

Tendons: test each one on its own

Look first

Hand relaxed, palm up: the fingers rest a bit more bent from index to little. A finger lying straighter than it should = flexor cut. You can see it even in a child or a drunk patient.

Can't cooperate? Gently bend the wrist back: the fingers should curl. One that doesn't = flexor cut. Photograph this side view; it's often the most useful picture for your senior.

Flexors

FDP: bends the fingertip

Hold the middle phalanx so the middle joint stays straight. "Bend just the tip." Do every finger.

FDS: bends the middle joint

Hold all the other fingers flat and straight. "Bend this finger." It should bend at the middle joint with a floppy tip.

Index: this test isn't reliable. Ask for a strong pinch with the thumb, pad to pad: tip stays straight = FDS working. Little: FDS is weak or missing in some people, so compare with the other hand.

FPL (thumb): hold the base of the thumb, "bend the tip".

Partial cuts

  • Moves fully but painful or weak against resistance = partial cut. Write exactly that, not "intact".
  • Gripping a knife or glass when cut? The tendon was cut with the finger bent, so the tendon cut sits further down the finger than the skin cut.
  • A wound right over a tendon usually gets explored anyway. Your job is to report accurately, not to rule it out.

Zones (tell your senior the zone)

How to write it: "R middle: FDP bends tip fully, no pain. FDS bends with floppy tip but painful and weak vs L. Middle lies straighter than ring at rest. Video sent."

Infection: Kanavel's signs

Flexor sheath infection: 4 signs

  1. Finger held slightly bent.
  2. Whole finger swollen like a sausage.
  3. Tender along the palm side of the finger, over the tendon.
  4. Pain when you gently straighten it (often the earliest sign).

Early on, not all 4 show. Even 1 or 2 with a swollen finger: call your senior. It can destroy the tendon within a day and usually needs OT.

Photos your senior can use

A close-up of a red blob is useless. Your senior needs to know which hand, which finger, which side and how bad.

Always 2 photos:
  1. Zoomed out: whole hand and wrist, fingers spread.
  2. Zoomed in: the wound fills about half the screen, sharp.
  • Clean it first. Wipe off the blood, remove the dressing.
  • Plain background (blue or green drape), not bloody sheets.
  • Good light, no shadow from your head. Flash makes wet wounds shine.
  • Phone held flat above the hand, not at an angle. Tap to focus. Move closer instead of zooming.
  • Ruler next to the wound for size.
  • Zoom into your own photo. If you can't tell what it is, your senior can't either.
  • Extras: side view of the resting hand (tendons), fist (rotation), both hands side by side (swelling), short videos of tendon tests.

Calling your senior

"35M, right-handed chef. Cut L index on broken glass 2 h ago, was gripping it. Lac 1.5 cm, palm side, radial side over the proximal phalanx (zone II). Pink, CRT 2 s. 2PD radial 12 mm, ulnar 4 mm (R 4/4), before LA. FDP full but painful against resistance, FDS OK. X-ray no glass. Photos + videos sent. ?Digital nerve ± partial FDP, for explore when?"
