# Logbook brainstorm: 30 lenses, 361 ideas (13 Oct 2026)

Thirty agents each looked at the logbook through one lens for about 4 minutes, with no product limits (outside the usual rules: scope, new options off by default, planning in batches). They read only the logbook code. **Nothing here is decided or agreed**: it is a pool of ideas to pick from, not a plan. Effort runs S/M/L/XL; "radical" runs 1–5.

## What kept coming up (across several lenses)

1. **Capture without typing.** Voice dictation into the existing suggest engine (speed, accessibility, ux-radical, ai-capture). A photo of the OT list with on-device OCR into List mode (ai-capture, onboarding, speed). Deep-link prefill URLs for Siri Shortcuts, NFC tags in theatre and widgets (native, speed). One-tap tally mode.
2. **A de-identification firewall.** One local gate that every capture path passes through before saving. It is the precondition for all the AI ideas.
3. **Supervisor confirmation loop.** Tag the consultant, or scan a QR code; the consultant confirms in 10 seconds with a feedback chip; faculty get teaching credit. Links to evals ("ask for an eval from this case") (social, faculty, evals-integration).
4. **Reflections made lighter.** A 2-minute seed at save time, grown later. An AI sparring partner that only asks questions, starting through the existing Connect Claude MCP (reflection, ai-coach).
5. **Forward-looking analytics.** Pace-to-target forecasts, "what to ask for next week", and monthly snapshots in summaries for cohort early warning (analytics-resident, programme, competitors).
6. **Roster link.** Today's list card from the resident's own roster row, plus an end-of-list nudge (roster, speed).
7. **Trust and privacy.** Zero-knowledge encrypted reflections. An append-only audit trail, with break-glass grants in place of standing owner write access (privacy, security).
8. **Simplify and speed up.** Lazy-load everything but the Case tab; ExcelJS and the demo seed are in the boot path (performance). A "settings amnesty" cutting ~95 toggles to ~10 (contrarian).
9. **Beyond APMES.** Requirements moved into a versioned "programme pack". A lifetime / consultant logbook with privileging evidence (multi-programme, portfolio).

## All ideas by lens

### speed

**Top pick:** Tally mode: log by counting, add details later. A one-tap grid of the resident's top tag groups saves a case that counts towards targets straight away, with the summary optional. It meets the 5-second goal for the majority who log only for APMES, and it reuses Same as last, tag groups and Plan for later.

- **Tally mode: log by counting, add details later** (effort M, radical 4/5). A full-screen grid of the resident's 6-8 most-used tag groups (from tag-group history plus Same as last). One tap saves a case with today's date and those tags and no summary, then fades to a '+1 GA ETT, undo' toast. Bare cases go into an 'add a line later' queue that reuses Plan for later. _Why:_ The APMES audit only needs the tags. Most residents log only for the requirements, so the smallest record that counts can be one tap, with the summary optional and added later.
- **Voice dictation straight into suggest()** (effort M, radical 3/5). A hold-to-talk mic on the case box uses on-device Web Speech, so 'seventy-two male, lap chole, GA ETT, difficult airway' is turned into text and fed live to the existing parseAge plus keyword suggest() engine, with the suggested tags shown as one-tap confirmations. Words often misheard in theatre ('ETT', 'CSE', 'TIVA') are mapped to shorthand. _Why:_ Gloved hands, a mask and a 30-second gap between cases suit voice better than typing. The suggestion engine is already there and only needs text to work on.
- **Predictive pre-fill by time, place and rota** (effort M, radical 3/5). When the Case tab opens, it pre-selects the most likely tag set from day of week, time, recent run of cases and (optionally) the posting from the NUH roster app, shown as a dimmed 'Likely: Obstetrics, Neuraxial', confirmed with one tap. Nothing is auto-ticked: the tags still need one deliberate tap. _Why:_ Residents on an obstetric or paediatric list log near-identical cases all day. Predicting the set turns 'pick tags' into 'confirm tags' without breaking the 'tags picked on purpose' principle.
- **Repeat ×N stepper on Same as last** (effort S, radical 2/5). A long press on the Same as last chip opens a stepper ('×3 today'), which saves N copies with an optional initials line for each (e.g. 'AB, CD, EF' split on commas). This builds on List mode. _Why:_ A list of 4 identical cataract or ECT cases is common, and logging them one by one is the most tedious path in the app.
- **Lock-screen and home-screen widget via a thin native wrapper** (effort XL, radical 5/5). Wrap the PWA (TWA on Android, a small Swift shell on iOS) to add a widget with three buttons (your top tag groups) and an iOS Live Activity during a theatre list showing 'Cases today: 5, tap to log'. A widget tap writes to a local queue that the PWA syncs. _Why:_ Getting to the app means unlock, find the app, wait for it to load. A widget removes that, which is the only way to get under 5 seconds from a locked phone.
- **Siri and Google Assistant shortcuts through URL actions** (effort S, radical 3/5). Expose deep links like ./#log?tags=GA,ETT&save=confirm and ./#log?text=..., and ship a ready-made iOS Shortcut ('Hey Siri, log a case') that dictates text and opens that URL. The app shows a single confirm sheet. _Why:_ This gives voice and lock-screen-ish logging with no native app, using only the existing hash routes and manifest shortcuts.
- **Richer share target: share text in, logged** (effort M, radical 2/5). The manifest already has a GET share_target. Make shared text open in a compact 'confirm and save' sheet with tags pre-suggested, instead of the full Case tab. Also accept POST of images or OCR text, so a shared theatre-list photo or WhatsApp handover line becomes a case draft. _Why:_ Residents already have case lines in messages and notes. Sharing straight in skips copy, switch app, paste.
- **Theatre-list camera import with on-device OCR** (effort L, radical 4/5). Photograph the day's printed theatre list; on-device OCR (Tesseract WASM) extracts procedure lines, removes names and hospital numbers before anything leaves the device, and loads them into Plan for later, so after each case you only tap 'done'. _Why:_ This moves the logging work to the start of the day: one photo, then one tap per case. Removing identifiers on the device is required for privacy.
- **Pending-case nudges at natural breaks** (effort M, radical 3/5). An opt-in local notification at the end of a session ('3 planned cases not confirmed', or at 5 pm 'Log today's cases?') with notification action buttons ('Same as last', 'Open') that save without opening the app. _Why:_ Most missed logs are forgotten, not slow. A one-tap action in the notification also counts as logging under 5 seconds.
- **Keyboard-only power path** (effort S, radical 2/5). In the case box, '/' plus shorthand (e.g. '/ga /ett /paeds') turns into tag chips inline, and Ctrl/Cmd+Enter saves. Typing 'lscs spinal' and pressing Enter saves when the suggestion confidence is above a threshold the user sets. _Why:_ Desktop and tablet users logging in batches after a shift never need to touch the mouse. The shorthand engine already maps words to APMES codes.
- **Wearable tap logger** (effort XL, radical 5/5). A watch complication (Wear OS tile or Apple Watch app) with your 3 tag groups: tap the wrist, the case is queued, and it syncs to the PWA queue later. Haptic confirmation; nothing typed. _Why:_ Phones are often in lockers or under gowns in theatre, while the watch is on the wrist. This is the most radical way to cut friction.
- **Chat-based logging through the existing MCP connector** (effort M, radical 4/5). Extend the logbook's MCP server so any assistant ('log 2 GA paeds tonsils today') can create cases, showing proposed tags and asking for confirmation before writing. _Why:_ For residents who already live in a chat assistant, logging becomes one sentence, using a connector the app already has.

### ai-capture

**Top pick:** Snap the OT list, get a draft day, gated by the de-identification firewall. Text recognised on the device from a photo, a share-sheet or a voice note goes into the existing Paste-a-list review with suggest.js tags proposed but not ticked. It removes most of the typing with the least new machinery, and it keeps both rules: nothing identifiable leaves the phone, and tags are picked on purpose.

- **Snap the OT list, get a draft day** (effort M, radical 3/5). Photograph the printed or whiteboard OT list. On-device OCR (Tesseract.js / WASM, or the browser's Shape/TextDetector where it exists) turns each row into a line that goes straight into the existing Paste-a-list (List mode) review. The raw image is thrown away at once and never uploaded. Each line arrives with suggest.js tags already proposed, but none ticked. _Why:_ Most of the effort in logging a list is re-typing it. List mode, initials detection and the suggest engine already exist, so OCR only needs to feed text into a pipeline that is already built.
- **De-identification firewall before anything leaves the device** (effort M, radical 2/5). Put a mandatory local pass (de-identify.js) between any capture (OCR, speech, paste) and the case box. It strips NRIC/FIN patterns, hospital numbers, dates of birth, full names (capitalised word pairs not in KEYWORDS), bed and ward numbers and phone numbers, and leaves only initials plus age/sex. It extends the existing ID-number check, and it shows what it removed so the resident can confirm. _Why:_ This is the precondition for every AI capture idea: a public, audit-sensitive app can't let PHI reach Firestore or a model. Making it the single gate keeps the guarantee testable.
- **Talk-through-the-case voice logging** (effort M, radical 3/5). A hold-to-talk mic on the Case tab using on-device Web Speech, or Whisper-tiny in WASM for offline use: '62 male, lap chole, GA ETT, difficult airway, video laryngoscope'. The transcript goes through the de-identification firewall into the case box, and live tag suggestions light up as you speak. A medical shorthand post-processor maps 'E-T-T' to ETT, 'six M O' to 6mo, and so on. _Why:_ Residents log between cases with gloves on or while walking. Voice turns a 60-second chore into 10 seconds, and it reuses the parseAge rules (6m vs 6mo).
- **End-of-day voice debrief that splits into cases** (effort L, radical 4/5). Dictate one rambling 2-minute summary of the whole list. A local LLM (WebLLM / Gemma-2B in WebGPU) segments it into N cases with initials, age/sex, procedure and anaesthetic technique, and shows them as List-mode drafts to accept, edit or drop. _Why:_ This matches how residents actually recall their day, in one go after the list, rather than case by case.
- **Anaesthetic chart photo to technique tags** (effort XL, radical 5/5). Photograph the anaesthetic record. A vision model finds the airway device, regional blocks, lines, drugs (vasopressor infusion, TIVA), the ASA box and the duration. These map to APMES tags as suggestions only, and the image never persists (or persists blurred, under the existing photo budget, if the resident wants it as a figure). _Why:_ The chart already records the skills that count towards targets (arterial line, CVC, DLT, neuraxial). Reading it catches tags that residents forget to tick.
- **Confidence-shaded tag proposals that are never auto-ticked** (effort S, radical 2/5). AI-proposed tags show as outlined chips with a confidence shade and the phrase that triggered them (e.g. 'DLT ← "double lumen"'). One tap confirms each, and 'accept all high-confidence' is a deliberate link that asks first. _Why:_ This keeps the owner's rule that tags are picked on purpose because they count towards credentialling, and makes the AI's reasoning visible and auditable.
- **Learned personal calibration from your own corrections** (effort S, radical 2/5). Every accept or reject of a proposed tag updates a per-user calibration table, layered over the global CALIBRATION in suggest.js and stored in settings. Your shorthand ('LMA' always means i-gel for you) then improves suggestions over time, with no server-side model. _Why:_ A cheap, private, on-device form of 'AI' that compounds. The calibrate() hook already exists.
- **Roster- and calendar-aware pre-fill** (effort M, radical 3/5). Using the NUH roster app on the same origin plus the date, propose tomorrow's 'Plan for later' skeleton: your posting (e.g. Neuro OT, KK paeds), so likely tag groups are pre-staged before the list starts. The OCR'd list then only fills in initials and procedures. _Why:_ It cuts the number of choices at capture time, and it ties together two apps the owner already runs.
- **Share-sheet / Web Share Target capture** (effort S, radical 2/5). Register the PWA as a share target, so a screenshot of the e-OT list, a WhatsApp list message or text copied from the EMR can be shared straight into the logbook. It passes through de-identification and opens List-mode review. _Why:_ This is zero-friction capture from wherever the list already is, without camera framing, and it's practical with today's PWA APIs.
- **Missing-case detective** (effort M, radical 3/5). Compare captured OT lists (as de-identified fingerprints: date + procedure + initials hash) against logged cases. It nudges 'You captured 7 cases on Tuesday's list but logged 5; these 2 are still drafts'. _Why:_ Under-logging is the main reason residents miss APMES targets, so closing the loop from capture to logged turns AI capture into completeness, not just speed.
- **Reflection seed from captured case** (effort M, radical 3/5). For a case marked ☆ Interesting, the captured transcript or chart extraction drafts the reflection's Case details and three Socratic prompts ('What would you do differently with the difficult airway?'). The resident writes the reflection itself. _Why:_ Reflections are the hardest APMES requirement to keep up with. The AI scaffolds the work without ghost-writing, so the learning value is kept.
- **Bring-your-own-model via the MCP connector** (effort M, radical 4/5). Offer an opt-in mode where the resident's own Claude/GPT client parses an already-de-identified text dump into cases through the existing MCP connector (server.mjs). The app ships no model or keys and only accepts structured case drafts back. _Why:_ Residents get powerful parsing at no hosting cost, without the app or programme becoming a data processor for clinical text.

### ai-coach

**Top pick:** Reflection sparring partner, released first through the existing Connect Claude MCP server and then inside the app. Residents struggle most with writing reflections. A coach that only asks questions keeps the resident as the author, and offering it through MCP prompts first tests demand with no backend, API keys or privacy risk.

- **Reflection sparring partner (in-editor Socratic coach)** (effort M, radical 3/5). Add a 'Coach me' button to the reflection editor. It reads the Case details and the draft learning points, then asks 3 to 5 escalating Socratic questions (what did you expect, what happened, why, what would you do differently, what is the evidence) and never writes the reflection itself. The resident's answers become optional learning points they can keep or delete. _Why:_ The 96 reflections are the hardest part of APMES to finish and the easiest to write badly. A coach that asks rather than writes keeps the resident as the author, which matters for the portfolio's credibility, and it turns a chore into actual learning.
- **60-second debrief right after Save** (effort S, radical 2/5). After a case is saved, especially one with high-yield tags or the Interesting star, show one optional question chosen from its tags (for example 'Obstetric GA: what was your plan if intubation had failed?'). The answer is saved as a Note linked to the case and can later be turned into a reflection with one tap. _Why:_ Learning is strongest in the minutes after a case, but nobody writes a reflection between cases. A one-line answer captures the insight before it is forgotten and fills the Reflections tab with ready material, without slowing logging (it is skippable and off by default).
- **Learning-gap radar from tags vs the curriculum** (effort M, radical 3/5). Compare the resident's tag counts, reflection headings, required sub-types and JR caps with the APMES requirements and EPA guidebook (already transcribed in apmes/), then give a plain-language diagnosis: 'You have 40 neuraxial cases but no reflection on a complication; no paediatric airway under age 1; 4 months since your last ICU case.' Each gap links to Plan for later or a reflection prompt. _Why:_ Analytics already shows the counts, but residents need someone to interpret them. Finding gaps by content, beyond just counts, is what a good educational supervisor does, and it can be computed mostly on the device.
- **Exam viva generator from your own cases** (effort L, radical 4/5). Turn the resident's logged cases into SOE (structured oral exam) style viva stems, de-identified and paraphrased: 'You are called to a 34-week parturient with...'. The coach then runs a timed oral, marks answers against a rubric and sends weak areas to spaced repetition. Residents can choose cases by tag, heading or the Interesting star. _Why:_ Questions built on cases the resident has actually seen stick far better than generic question banks, and they make the logbook something residents open for exam preparation, not just for the audit.
- **Spaced-repetition cards mined from learning points** (effort M, radical 3/5). Each learning point in a completed reflection is turned into one or two flashcards (with the resident's approval) and reviewed on an SM-2 or FSRS schedule from a small daily 'Review 5' card on the Glance or Analytics screen. _Why:_ Residents write 96 reflections' worth of learning points and then never look at them again. Spacing turns that portfolio paperwork into knowledge they keep, using content they wrote themselves.
- **Reflection quality mirror (Gibbs/Kolb rubric feedback)** (effort M, radical 2/5). Score a draft reflection privately against a reflective-writing rubric (description vs analysis vs action plan; specific vs generic; evidence cited), shown as a small radar with one concrete suggestion. It also flags privacy risks such as names, NRIC-like strings or exact dates before export. _Why:_ Faculty see many reflections that only describe what happened. Feedback before submission raises quality, and combining it with the identifier check reinforces the public-safe and patient-privacy rules.
- **Pattern-spotting across your cases ('you keep...')** (effort L, radical 4/5). An occasional longitudinal insight from case summaries and notes: 'Three of your last five difficult airways were in BMI over 40 at night', or 'You often mention hypotension after spinal for caesarean; here is a reflection prompt on prophylactic vasopressors'. Each insight comes with its evidence (the linked cases). _Why:_ No human supervisor reads a resident's whole logbook. Patterns across cases show habits and blind spots that a single case never reveals, which is the core of deliberate practice.
- **Supervisor-ready case brief and EPA nudge** (effort M, radical 3/5). Before an evals (workplace-based assessment) request, the coach drafts a short brief from the linked case and reflection: what the resident did, the EPA it maps to, and what feedback they want. It also suggests which recent cases would make good EPA assessments that the resident has not yet requested. _Why:_ It connects the logbook and evals apps, which share one origin and one Firebase project, and it makes faculty time more productive because the requests arrive with focused questions.
- **Coach runs through the existing Connect Claude MCP server** (effort S, radical 2/5). Start with no in-app model at all: add MCP prompts and tools to logbook/mcp (for example 'socratic_debrief(caseId)', 'gap_report', 'viva_from_tags') so residents who already connect Claude get the coach today, with read-only access under the Firestore rules. _Why:_ This is the cheapest way to test whether residents want an AI coach. It avoids a server-side LLM proxy, API keys in a static site and the question of sending reflections to a third party, because the resident brings their own Claude.
- **On-device private model mode** (effort XL, radical 5/5). Run a small LLM in the browser with WebGPU (WebLLM or a Gemma/Phi-class model cached by the service worker) so the coaching questions, gap phrasing and privacy checks never leave the phone. Users who allow it could switch to a cloud model. _Why:_ Reflections are private (the rules hide them even from the owner), so an on-device model is the only option with no privacy trade-off. It fits the static PWA, which has no backend.
- **Cohort-anonymised 'what peers learned' feed** (effort XL, radical 5/5). Residents who opt in share de-identified learning points by tag. The coach summarises them as 'Residents in your intake found these lessons in obstetric GA' and asks whether the resident agrees, disagrees or has seen it too. _Why:_ It turns lonely logging into collective learning and passes tacit knowledge between years. Faculty / PCs could see the common themes to plan teaching.
- **Voice debrief on the walk back from theatre** (effort L, radical 4/5). Hold to talk: the resident speaks for one minute after a case. The app transcribes it on the device, removes identifiers, sorts it into case summary, tags and learning points, and asks one follow-up question aloud. _Why:_ Typing is the main barrier to reflecting on a phone between lists. Speaking is faster and more honest, and the coach's follow-up question turns a ramble into a structured reflection.

### reflection

**Top pick:** Two-minute seed, then grow: capture a 3-prompt reflection seed (typed, or later spoken) the moment a case is saved or starred, link it to the case, and bring it back through the existing Reflection to-dos to expand later. It removes the biggest cost of 96 reflections, which is remembering the case, and builds on what the app already has (☆ Interesting, drafts, caseId linking, to-dos).

- **Two-minute seed, then grow** (effort S, radical 2/5). Right after a case is saved or starred ☆ Interesting, offer a 3-field "seed" (what surprised me / what I'd do differently / one thing to look up), stored as a draft reflection linked by caseId. Weeks later, Reflection to-dos brings the seed back to be expanded into full learning points, with the case summary already filled in. _Why:_ Most of the work of writing 96 reflections is remembering the case. Capturing the insight while it is fresh, in under two minutes, turns the later write-up into editing rather than recall, and the existing drafts and to-dos give it somewhere to live.
- **Voice reflection with on-device transcript** (effort M, radical 3/5). Hold to record a 60–90 s voice memo after the case (walking back from theatre). It is transcribed on the device (Web Speech API, or Whisper-in-WASM) into the summary and learning points, and the audio is thrown away so nothing identifiable is stored. A de-identification pass flags spoken names, NRIC-like strings and dates before saving. _Why:_ Residents talk about cases all the time but rarely write them up. Speaking is 3–4 times faster than thumb-typing on a phone, and dropping the audio keeps the privacy model as it is now.
- **Socratic prompt deck per heading** (effort S, radical 2/5). Each reflection heading (Obstetrics, Paeds, Airway…) gets a small deck of sharp, specific prompts ("What was your plan B for the airway, and when would you have switched?"). Tap a prompt and it becomes a learning-point heading. Prompts rotate so that 96 reflections don't read the same. _Why:_ A blank "learning points" box breeds generic text. Specific prompts get specific thinking, give faculty better material, and make the 4th Obstetrics reflection different from the 1st.
- **EPA and competency linking with evidence map** (effort L, radical 3/5). Tag each learning point with the EPAs or competency domains it shows (data already transcribed in apmes/ for evals). A heat-map shows which EPAs have rich reflective evidence and which are thin, and the evals app can link back to the reflection as supporting evidence for an assessment. _Why:_ Reflections then count as evidence of progress towards entrustment, not only towards the 96, and the logbook, reflections and evals become one portfolio story for the CCC.
- **Peer reflection circles (opt-in, anonymised)** (effort L, radical 4/5). A resident can share one de-identified reflection with a small circle (their intake or a chosen buddy). Peers reply with one "I had a similar case…" or "Have you considered…" comment, and the author can fold good replies in as an extra learning point. Faculty can run a monthly "reflection rounds" from what was shared. _Why:_ Reflection done alone is a chore; reflection done together teaches people. This turns private paperwork into a learning community and gives faculty a teaching resource without extra content to write.
- **AI reflection coach (Gibbs/Kolb depth feedback)** (effort M, radical 4/5). An optional coach reads a draft and rates its depth (descriptive → analytical → transformative on a Gibbs or Kolb style ladder), then asks one or two follow-up questions rather than rewriting anything. It never writes the text, and a "show me my own words only" check keeps it honest. _Why:_ Residents rarely get feedback on reflections until the portfolio review, when it's too late. Instant, specific nudges towards deeper reflection improve quality without handing the writing to AI.
- **Reflection threads: one theme across many cases** (effort M, radical 3/5). Allow a "thread" that links several cases and reflections about one recurring theme (for example "difficult spinals in obese parturients" or "handover failures"), with a short meta-reflection on what changed over time. Under the one-patient-per-reflection rule, each linked reflection still counts once. _Why:_ Growth shows best across cases, not within one. Threads show a learning arc that portfolio reviewers love and residents can actually see.
- **Lighter formats: Rapid, Critical Incident, Compare & Contrast** (effort M, radical 2/5). Offer templates beyond free text and points: a "Rapid" format (3 bullets, at least 150 words), "Critical incident" (what happened, my role, human factors, system fix) and "Compare two cases" (pick two logged cases and contrast the management). Each exports to the same Word structure. _Why:_ Not every case deserves an essay. Choosing a format that fits the case cuts the friction, and the compare format uses the logbook's own case history to drive deeper thinking.
- **Spaced revisit: "6 months on" addendum** (effort S, radical 3/5). Six months after a reflection is completed, the app resurfaces it with one question: "Would you manage this differently now? What have you learned since?" A short dated addendum is added. The Word export can show addenda as "Further reflection". _Why:_ It turns reflection from a one-off write-up into spaced retrieval and shows longitudinal growth, which is the real point of a reflective portfolio.
- **Auto-drafted case context from the logbook** (effort S, radical 2/5). When writing a reflection, pull related history from the resident's own logbook: "You've done 14 similar cases; your last 3 had these tags; first one was R1". It is added as an optional context line or prompt ("What's different now from your first?"). _Why:_ It grounds the reflection in the resident's real experience curve and gives them a ready opening, using data the app already holds.
- **Faculty micro-feedback and "reflection of the month"** (effort L, radical 4/5). Faculty / PCs (who already see count summaries) can, with the resident's consent, read a shared reflection and leave a one-tap reaction plus a one-line comment. The programme highlights an anonymised standout reflection each month as an example. _Why:_ Residents write 96 reflections that almost nobody reads until the end. Feedback during the year makes the effort feel seen and models what good looks like.
- **Reflection-as-teaching output** (effort M, radical 4/5). One tap turns a finished reflection into a short teaching card (key points, pearl, references) that can be shared with juniors or exported to a departmental teaching bank or Anki deck. _Why:_ It gives each reflection a second use beyond the portfolio. Reflections become teaching material, which motivates quality and builds a department knowledge base.

### analytics-resident

**Top pick:** Theatre-list matchmaker ('what to ask for next week'): it reuses the tested paceForecast/goneQuiet helpers and turns passive analytics into a concrete weekly action residents can take, without adding any friction to logging.

- **Theatre-list matchmaker: 'what to ask for next week'** (effort M, radical 3/5). Turn paceForecast + goneQuiet (js/analytics.js) into a ranked weekly ask list, such as 'ask for 2 paeds airways and 1 obstetric GA'. Each line gives the reason (off pace, gone quiet, deadline 30 Jun) and a one-tap 'copy as message to roster/consultant' text. Optionally, if the resident pastes next week's posting or list, only asks that the posting can actually deliver are shown. _Why:_ Pace bars show what is wrong but not what to do about it. The thing a resident can actually change is what they ask for in Monday's huddle, so the analytics only pay off if they lead to that ask.
- **Probabilistic completion forecast (fan chart)** (effort M, radical 3/5). Replace the single 'about 4 months at your pace' with a Monte Carlo (or Poisson) forecast per tag and overall. It shows the chance of meeting each milestone by 30 June, such as '72% likely on time', and a fan chart of the projected cumulative count. It allows for rotation seasonality, so cases arrive in bursts on the posting that offers them. _Why:_ Linear pace misleads for rare, posting-bound tags like obstetrics or cardiac. A probability tells the resident honestly whether to worry now or wait for the posting, and false 'off pace' alarms drop.
- **Posting-aware forecast from rotation history** (effort L, radical 4/5). Learn from the anonymised cohort summaries (Totals already loads them) which tags each posting typically yields per month, such as 'residents on Paeds log ~6 regionals/month'. The forecast then becomes 'you'll likely close this gap in your Feb–Apr KKH posting', not 'you're behind'. _Why:_ Most 'behind' tags are really 'not posted there yet'. Calling these 'expected later' removes anxiety and lets the resident put effort on real gaps.

### offline

**Top pick:** Per-case sync receipts plus persistent storage: show saved / waiting / confirmed on every row with a \"safe to close\" line, and call navigator.storage.persist() with an iOS eviction warning. Both are small, build on writes.js job states and Firestore metadata that already exist, and close the most likely real-world cause of lost cases: the phone locked or storage evicted before sync, without the resident knowing.

- **Per-case sync receipts in every list** (effort S, radical 1/5). Give each case, reflection and note row a small three-state mark: saved on this phone, waiting to send, confirmed by the server. The data already exists in the writes.js job states and in Firestore hasPendingWrites metadata. Add a header line such as "3 waiting, safe to close once ticked". _Why:_ Residents leave theatre and lock the phone without knowing whether a case left the device. Today the who-alert banner says only that something is unsynced. A tick on each row builds trust and shows which cases are at risk.
- **Ask for persistent storage and warn about eviction** (effort S, radical 1/5). Call navigator.storage.persist() after the first case is logged, and show storage quota and persistence state in Settings. On iOS Safari outside an installed app, warn that pending cases can be evicted after 7 days of no use, and nudge the resident to install to the home screen. Nothing in logbook/js calls storage.persist yet. _Why:_ Both the IndexedDB recovery store and Firestore's persistent cache can be evicted silently. The never-lose-a-case promise rests on storage the browser is allowed to delete.
- **Three-way field merge instead of whole-record conflicts** (effort M, radical 2/5). commitRecord already sends the editor's base version, so instead of rejecting with write-conflict, diff base to local and base to remote field by field. Auto-merge changes that don't overlap (for example, tags on one device and details on the other, or tag sets combined). Only edits to the same field reach the conflict screen, shown as a side-by-side pick with a "keep both" option. _Why:_ Residents who use a phone in theatre and a laptop at home will hit conflicts that are not real disagreements. Fewer conflict screens means fewer stuck or abandoned records.
- **Theatre capture inbox that needs no SDK or sign-in** (effort M, radical 3/5). Add a tiny capture path served by the service worker: date, initials, codes and free text written straight to an IndexedDB inbox. It works even when the Firebase chunk is not cached, the auth token expired offline, or the app is half-loaded. The full app later moves inbox items into proper cases, with a review step. _Why:_ The worst failures happen when signal is poor and the app shell or auth can't start. A capture path with no dependencies means a case can always be written down within 5 seconds.
- **Background drain from the service worker** (effort L, radical 3/5). Register a Background Sync (and Periodic Sync where supported) task. The service worker reads the pending queue from IndexedDB and commits jobs through the Firestore REST API, using a short-lived token cached with the job, then posts results back to open tabs. _Why:_ Today the queue drains only while a tab is open and online, so a case logged in a basement theatre can wait until the next time the app is opened. Background drain gets it out as soon as the phone finds signal.
- **Silent-loss audit (sync integrity check)** (effort M, radical 3/5). Keep a per-month count and hash of case IDs and update times on the server (like the noteIndex meta doc) and compare it on device. A weekly or on-demand "Check my logbook" reports mismatches: missing locally, missing on the server, or changed. It also cross-checks the recycle bin and the recovery store. _Why:_ Loss is usually silent: a cleared cache, a bad rules deploy, a bug in a batch import. Detecting drift before portfolio submission is worth more than any recovery tool.
- **Offline duplicate detector across devices** (effort S, radical 2/5). When cases from two devices sync, flag likely duplicates (same date and initials, overlapping codes, created within hours) in a gentle merge card: keep one, merge the tags, or keep both. Also check at entry time against the local cache. _Why:_ With poor signal, residents often re-log a case on a second device because the first one "didn't save". That inflates targets and spoils progress counts.
- **Phone-to-phone or phone-to-laptop handoff with no internet** (effort L, radical 5/5). Encode the pending queue as animated QR frames, or use WebRTC on the local network, so another device that does have signal (a colleague's, or the theatre PC) can carry the resident's unsynced cases. The payload is encrypted with a key only the resident's account can open, and the receiving device just relays it. _Why:_ In theatre the resident's phone may be the only device with no connection. Moving data by sight or local network gets around dead zones without waiting for the resident to leave.
- **Out-of-band escape hatch: an encrypted bundle to yourself** (effort M, radical 4/5). If anything has been unsynced for more than N hours, or a sync failed, offer one tap: "Send a safety copy to myself". This makes a compact, encrypted, de-identified bundle (cases are tiny: date, initials, codes) shared through the OS share sheet to email, WhatsApp or Notes. Paste or open it in the app later to restore. _Why:_ A last resort that doesn't depend on Firestore being reachable or healthy. Residents already trust messaging apps to send things eventually.
- **Local-first rewrite: IndexedDB as the source of truth** (effort XL, radical 5/5). Make the device's own store canonical and treat Firestore as a replica, using an op-log or CRDT per record (Automerge or Yjs style, or RxDB/PowerSync-like replication) with hybrid logical clocks to order edits. The UI never waits on Firestore and conflicts become mathematically mergeable. _Why:_ This removes a class of Firestore offline-cache quirks the notes already work around (fromCache never turning false without includeMetadataChanges, multi-tab cache edge cases), and makes multi-device editing native rather than patched.
- **Hybrid logical clocks and server timestamps for ordering** (effort M, radical 3/5). Stamp every write with a hybrid logical clock plus device ID, and record serverTimestamp on commit. Order history, "last 100 saved versions" and conflict choices by that clock instead of the device's Date.now(). _Why:_ Hospital phones and shared devices often have the wrong clock, and offline edits from two devices can then be ordered wrongly. Recovery and conflict views need a trustworthy order.
- **Chaos and property tests for the write queue** (effort M, radical 2/5). createWriteQueue already accepts store, backend, online and delay as parameters. Add a fuzz harness with random offline flips, backend failures and conflicts, tab kills in the middle of a drain, account switches and storage quota errors. Assert that no job is lost or duplicated and that pending jobs never expire. Add a Playwright "airplane mode roulette" on top of recovery.mjs. _Why:_ Reliability claims need adversarial proof. Edge cases between the generation counters, account switches and failed-then-identical retries are exactly where cases quietly disappear.

Every read by an admin, PD or faculty member (submissions, submitted images, summaries) appends an entry to a resident-visible access log (`logbooks/{email}/access`). Each entry records who looked, what they looked at, when and why (the reviewer picks a purpose). It shows in More → Privacy as a timeline. _Why:_ Residents currently can't tell whether a PC opened their submission. Making access visible deters snooping and builds trust that the programme looks only when it needs to.
- **Close the app-owner read path on cases** (effort M, radical 3/5). `isAppOwner()` can read and write every resident's logbook and cases (used for maintenance such as 'Fix initials in older cases'). Replace it with time-boxed, resident-approved 'break glass' grants: the resident taps Allow for 24 hours, the grant is logged, and then it expires. _Why:_ A standing god-mode read on case logs is the biggest gap between the privacy story and the rules. Consent-based maintenance access fixes that without losing the one-off repair tools.
- **Privacy ledger / data passport screen** (effort M, radical 2/5). One screen in plain language listing every place the resident's data lives: Firestore collections, Dropbox or Drive backups, local drafts, the recovery store, the MCP connector and shared summaries. For each place it shows who can read it (from the rules), how long it is kept, and one-tap delete or export. _Why:_ PDPA access and correction rights, made concrete. Most residents have no idea that summaries are visible to faculty or that drafts sit in IndexedDB. This screen is useful now and is a strong trust signal.
- **Granular, revocable sharing of summaries** (effort L, radical 3/5). Summaries are readable by all admins, PDs, members and active faculty. Instead, let each resident choose who sees what: programme only, their named supervisor, or peers in their intake (aggregated only). Grants show as expiring 'share cards' they can revoke. _Why:_ Turns a blanket visibility rule into consent the resident controls, which is the heart of PDPA's consent obligation and of resident ownership.
- **On-device PHI guard** (effort M, radical 3/5). A local detector (regex plus a small on-device model) flags NRIC/FIN numbers, MRN patterns, full names, dates of birth and ward/bed numbers in the case summary, reflections and photo EXIF before saving. It suggests de-identified rewrites, and nothing leaves the device for the check. _Why:_ The app's promise is de-identified cases, and the existing ID-number check is a start. A stronger, always-on guard protects residents from accidental PDPA breaches that would hurt both them and the programme.
- **Local-first mode with the cloud as an optional relay** (effort XL, radical 5/5). A profile where all data lives on the device (IndexedDB/OPFS), and sync runs between the resident's own devices through end-to-end-encrypted CRDT updates relayed by Firestore or WebRTC. Only the APMES submission is ever sent in plaintext. _Why:_ Residents literally own their data. It also makes the app resilient if the programme or the Firebase project goes away, and the drafts, recovery store and backup targets are already halfway there.
- **Verifiable, signed portfolio exports** (effort L, radical 4/5). Sign the Word/Excel export with a hash and timestamp (a resident key plus a programme countersign, or a public transparency log such as Sigstore Rekor), so the programme can check that a portfolio hasn't been altered, without the app needing to keep the data. _Why:_ Provenance replaces surveillance: the programme can trust the logbook without central read access, which allows a more private architecture.
- **Graduation data handover and auto-expiry** (effort M, radical 3/5). When a resident leaves the programme, guide them through a 'take your logbook with you' bundle: an open JSON + Markdown + Word file, encrypted if they choose. Then schedule deletion of their cloud copy, with reminders, a configurable retention period and a deletion receipt. _Why:_ Covers PDPA's retention limitation obligation and shows that the logbook belongs to the resident across their career, not to the institution.
- **Reflection 'sealed envelope' and draft privacy** (effort M, radical 3/5). Let residents mark a reflection as 'private journal', so it can never be submitted or exported to Word unless they explicitly unseal it, with a lock icon and a separate passcode or biometric unlock on shared devices. Drafts in localStorage or IndexedDB are encrypted at rest and wiped on sign-out. _Why:_ Honest reflection on errors and distress needs psychological safety. Hospital workstations and shared phones are a real exposure.
- **Privacy-preserving cohort analytics** (effort L, radical 4/5). Replace per-resident summaries for intake comparison ('Compare with your intake') with differentially private aggregates, or k-anonymous buckets computed by a Cloud Function. A resident sees how they compare without anyone seeing an individual peer's counts. _Why:_ Comparison motivates residents, but per-person summaries readable by many roles leak who is behind. Differential privacy keeps the benefit without the exposure.
- **Public trust page and threat model** (effort S, radical 2/5). Publish a short, plain-English 'How your logbook is protected' page: the rules in human terms, what Google and the programme can or can't see, an incident-response promise, and a changelog of rules deploys (linked from the deploy workflow). It is reviewed by a DPO. _Why:_ Trust is earned through transparency. An open-source app can show its rules publicly, which is a differentiator against opaque institutional systems like MedHub.

### data-model

**Top pick:** Parse the case summary into structured fields: extend the age and sex parser so it also pulls ASA, technique, airway grade, block and urgency from text residents already type, and save them as optional chips you can remove. It adds rich structured data with no extra fields, so it keeps the 'frictionless logging first' principle, and it lays the groundwork for the facet layer, the crosswalks and the analytics.

- **Parse the case summary into structured fields, don't make people type them** (effort M, radical 3/5). Extend the parser that already reads age and sex from the case box (detectAgeSex, the '6m' and '3mo' rules) so it also pulls out ASA ('ASA 3E'), technique ('GA ETT', 'SAB', 'CSE', 'LMA'), airway grade ('CL2b', 'VL'), block type ('ISB', 'ESP') and urgency ('E', 'emerg'). The results are saved as an optional `facets` object on the case and shown as removable chips, just like the age and sex preview. _Why:_ Residents get rich, queryable data (ASA mix, GA vs regional, difficult-airway counts) with no extra fields to fill in, so 'frictionless logging first' still holds. The summary text stays the source of truth.
- **Facet layer: typed tags with dimensions** (effort M, radical 3/5). Turn personal tags (`u:`) into typed facets with a dimension (Technique, Airway, Line, Block, Complication, Supervision, Role) and an optional value (e.g. Airway=VL, CL3). APMES `c:` tags stay as they are. The tag editor CSV gains a 'Dimension' column, and Analytics can then pivot on any dimension. _Why:_ The tag system already lets personal tags link to APMES codes. Giving them a dimension makes a flat list of tags into a real data model, without a schema migration or forced fields.
- **Programme-neutral taxonomy with crosswalk files** (effort XL, radical 5/5). Treat CATEGORIES as just one 'programme pack'. Each case stores neutral clinical facets (specialty, procedure, age band, technique), and a crosswalk file maps those facets to APMES codes, RCoA ILCA, ANZCA, ACGME case categories or a future APMES v9. Targets and Progress are computed from the crosswalk, not from the hard-coded `cats` codes. _Why:_ When APMES revises its categories (retired codes like 15ii, 20iv and 26iv already exist), a resident's history is re-scored automatically. The logbook also becomes portable for fellows going overseas or for exam applications.
- **SNOMED CT / OPCS / ICD-10-PCS procedure coding, behind the scenes** (effort L, radical 4/5). Ship a small offline lookup of common anaesthesia-relevant procedures (about 500 SNOMED CT concepts, with ICD-10-PCS and OPCS cross-references), matched from the summary in the same way keywords.js matches tokens. Each case optionally stores a `proc` concept id. The resident never sees the codes unless they ask. _Why:_ Coded procedures allow precise de-duplication, research audits, comparison with theatre system data and future interoperability, all without changing how residents type.
- **Retrain keywords.js into a multi-label facet suggester** (effort M, radical 3/5). Today keywords.js maps tokens to P(category) from 15,942 old cases, with calibration. Run the same pipeline for the new facets (technique, airway, block) and for personal tags, trained on each user's own history on the device, so suggestions learn each resident's shorthand. _Why:_ The suggestion engine is the app's quiet superpower. Personalising it per user and extending it to facets makes richer data almost free to log.
- **Complications and critical incidents as first-class events** (effort M, radical 3/5). Add an optional 'events' list per case (desaturation, CICO, anaphylaxis, LAST, accidental dural puncture, failed block, awareness) using a standard taxonomy such as the ANZCA/AIMS or SALG-style lists, each with a severity grade. One tap on '!' in the case box opens the list. Events link to a reflection and feed a personal M&M dashboard. _Why:_ Complications are where most learning happens, and they are what supervisors and portfolios ask about. Linking an event to a reflection closes the loop between logging and learning.
- **Procedure sub-logs: airway, lines, blocks with outcome** (effort L, radical 4/5). Give each case optional 'procedure entries' (ETT, VL, FONA, art line, CVC, PICC, ISB, ESP, epidural), each with attempts, success, ultrasound use and supervision level. The block categories (26 to 29) and challenging airway (12) are then counted from these entries rather than from tags alone. _Why:_ It gives residents and PCs real competence curves (success rate and attempts over time), which are much stronger evidence for EPA 3 and EPA 4 than raw counts.
- **Supervision and autonomy level per case, mapped to EPA entrustment** (effort S, radical 2/5). Add a single optional 1 to 5 entrustment scale per case (observed, direct, indirect, distant, supervising others) that maps to the evals app's EPA entrustment levels. Analytics then shows autonomy by category over time. _Why:_ Counts don't show readiness. Autonomy trajectories line up the logbook with the evals app on the same origin and could support EPA sign-off decisions.
- **Versioned taxonomy with migrations and 'as of' scoring** (effort M, radical 3/5). Give CATEGORIES and tag sets a version, with migration rules (e.g. 15ii folded into 15, 20iv retired), and store on each case which taxonomy version it was logged under. Progress can be computed 'as of' any version, and an admin can publish a new version that re-maps old cases with a preview of the changes. _Why:_ Retired codes are already piling up. Explicit versioning prevents silent drift when the programme changes its rules and keeps old portfolios defensible.
- **Graph model: patients, episodes and cases** (effort L, radical 5/5). Allow cases to be linked into an 'episode' (same de-identified patient across pre-op clinic, theatre, ICU and the acute pain round) using a local-only pseudonymous patient key that is hashed on the device and never synced in plain form. Reflections and unique-patient rules (already in reflections.js) then use this graph instead of initials matching. _Why:_ It models how anaesthesia actually works (perioperative journeys), makes the 'unique patients' check reliable, and enables continuity-of-care learning.
- **Open logbook schema and FHIR-ish export** (effort M, radical 4/5). Publish a JSON Schema for a case (facets, procedures, events, codes, programme crosswalk) and an export that emits FHIR Procedure-like resources or plain JSON-LD alongside the Word and Excel exports. The read-only MCP server would expose the same schema. _Why:_ A documented schema makes the data portable across programmes, tools and AI assistants, and lets other hospitals reuse the app.
- **Cohort-level taxonomy analytics for PCs** (effort L, radical 4/5). With the facet layer in place, the Faculty / PCs profile gets anonymised cohort heatmaps: case mix by posting, technique share, block diversity, complication rates. These are aggregated in rules-safe summary docs and never show individual cases. _Why:_ PCs can spot postings that under-deliver exposure (e.g. no CSEs at a site) and rebalance rotations. That makes the logbook a programme-quality tool, not only a compliance tracker.

### research

**Top pick:** Opt-in event micro-tags with counts-only aggregation through the existing summaries/ pipeline. The logbook already holds a trusted case-mix denominator, so a one-tap numerator (complication or event tags, never counted towards targets) unlocks complication rates, audit cycles, CUSUM curves and cohort research for little build cost and low privacy risk.

- **Opt-in 'Event' micro-tag layer for complications** (effort M, radical 3/5). Add a small built-in set of programme-wide event tags, each one tap (difficult airway, unplanned ICU, aspiration, LAST, anaphylaxis, awareness, dural puncture, PONV rescue, cancelled after induction). They live beside APMES tags but never count towards targets. They feed a per-resident and per-cohort complication rate, with the denominator taken from the cases already logged. _Why:_ The logbook already holds the denominator, which most departments lack: a reliable count of cases by type. Adding a numerator turns the logbook into an incident-rate instrument with no new forms, and it keeps the 'no extra structured fields' spirit because the events are just tags.
- **Extend summaries/ into an anonymised QI aggregate** (effort M, radical 3/5). Today the summaries/ docs hold only counts per tag. Extend them with counts of tag co-occurrences (e.g. GA+paeds+difficult airway), monthly bins and event-tag counts, never case text. Faculty / PCs get a 'Programme QI' view in Analytics with small-cell suppression (cells under 5 hidden). _Why:_ This reuses the exact pipeline Case logs and Totals already trust (counts only, no case details), so privacy risk stays low while the programme gains a live epidemiology of what its residents actually do.
- **Audit-cycle builder over saved searches** (effort L, radical 4/5). A faculty member defines an audit standard as an Advanced-search query plus a target, e.g. 'tag:spinal tag:caesarean' with the event tag 'converted to GA' kept under 5%. The app shows the cohort rate for cycle 1, sets a date for the re-audit, then draws the closed loop as a before/after chart with the change made in between noted. _Why:_ Closing the audit loop is a training requirement that residents struggle to evidence. Search-ops already parses this kind of query, so the audit becomes one saved object instead of a spreadsheet project.
- **Personal CUSUM / learning curves per procedure** (effort M, radical 4/5). For procedures with a success or failure flag (e.g. 'block failed', 'ETT 1st pass' sub-tags), plot a CUSUM learning curve per resident against accepted failure rates. Show the point where the resident crossed into competence. _Why:_ CUSUM is the established, publishable method for procedural competence in anaesthesia. It turns a raw tally of numbers into evidence of skill and gives the Faculty / PCs profile an objective tool for credentialling.
- **Federated cohort study mode with consent** (effort XL, radical 5/5). Residents tick a study consent (per study, revocable). Their de-identified tag vectors (date month, tags, event tags, no text, no initials) go to a separate study collection with k-anonymity checks, and approved researchers export a CSV. The protocol, ethics reference and data dictionary are shown in the app. _Why:_ A multi-year cohort of residents' case mix and complication exposure is publishable material (training exposure, effect of COVID or rotation changes, gender/case-mix equity). The logbook is the only place this data exists in structured form.
- **Turn the 'Interesting' star into an M&M case pipeline** (effort M, radical 3/5). Starred cases get an optional 'Bring to M&M / teaching' flag. Faculty see a queue of anonymised case summaries (initials stripped, dates coarsened to the month) and turn them into morbidity-and-mortality or teaching sessions, with credit back to the resident's reflection. _Why:_ The data already marks the cases residents find educational. Routing them to departmental learning closes the loop between individual reflection and organisational QI.
- **Free-text mining of case summaries, on the device** (effort M, radical 3/5). Run keyword/NLP extraction on the device over case summaries (e.g. 'desat', 'failed', 'bronchospasm', 'regurg', 'GVL x2'), building on the keywords.js and suggest.js machinery. It suggests event tags afterwards, and only counts leave the device. _Why:_ Residents already write the complications into the free text; they just never tag them. Retrospective extraction recovers QI data from the existing backlog without changing logging habits.
- **Exposure-gap and equity analytics** (effort M, radical 3/5). Compare case-mix distributions across residents in a year (Gini or entropy per tag) and flag systematic gaps, such as one posting never giving obstetric exposure, or residents on a shorter rotation getting fewer paediatric cases. Show it to PCs as a heatmap of rotation by tag. _Why:_ This turns the logbook into a QI tool for the training programme itself, giving evidence to fix roster and rotation design rather than blaming individual residents.
- **Programme-level annual report generator** (effort M, radical 2/5). One button for PCs produces a de-identified PDF or Word annual report: cases by tag, median per resident, event-tag rates, reflection completion, year-on-year trends, and charts ready for an accreditation review or a poster. _Why:_ It saves PCs days of manual collation and makes the programme's data visible and citable, the first step towards a publication.
- **Link with evals for training-outcome research** (effort L, radical 5/5). Join the logbook exposure counts with the evals (workplace-based assessment) entrustment levels in the same Firebase project. This answers how many cases of X it takes before EPA Y is entrusted, and shows the relationship at cohort level. _Why:_ Both apps share apmes-logbook, so this dataset is unique and valuable: dose-response curves between case volume and assessed competence are a recognised gap in the literature on competency-based medical education.
- **Department-facing benchmarks against registries** (effort L, radical 4/5). Map APMES tags to national or international registry categories (e.g. NAP audits, ASA activity data) so cohort rates of rare events (difficult airway, LAST, anaphylaxis) appear next to published incidence. _Why:_ It gives context to whether a programme's exposure to critical events is adequate for training, and supports simulation planning for the events residents rarely meet.
- **Snap QI pulse surveys tied to logging** (effort M, radical 4/5). Faculty can attach a time-limited one-question prompt to a tag, e.g. for 4 weeks after each 'tag:spinal' case: 'Was a post-op headache follow-up done? Y/N'. It is answered in one tap after Save, can be skipped, and only aggregates are visible. _Why:_ This collects prospective QI data at the point of care with almost no friction, enabling rapid PDSA cycles across the whole resident body.

### learning-science

**Top pick:** Case Flashbacks: spaced retrieval from your own cases. It reuses summaries residents already write and the ☆ Interesting bookmark, needs no extra logging fields, and turns a credentialling tally into daily, personalised retrieval practice. It is also the base that the viva questions, pearls, calibration and the exam-countdown ideas all plug into.

- **Case Flashbacks: spaced retrieval from your own cases** (effort M, radical 3/5). Each day, pick 1-3 of the resident's own logged cases (☆ Interesting ones first) at expanding intervals (1, 3, 7, 21, 60 days). Show only the case summary and hide the tags and reflection, then ask 'What was your plan? What would you change?' The resident types or thinks an answer, reveals what they actually wrote, and rates their recall (again / hard / good). That rating sets the next interval, as in Anki's SM-2. _Why:_ Residents already write rich summaries and then never look at them again. Retrieval practice on cases they personally managed is the most memorable material they have, and it reuses data they already entered, so logging costs nothing extra.
- **Curriculum heatmap: cases mapped to exam syllabus topics** (effort L, radical 3/5). Map every APMES tag and personal tag (and keywords.js matches on the case text) to the exam syllabus topics, e.g. MMed/FANZCA/EDAIC-style domains: airway, cardiac physiology, obstetrics, pharmacology of volatiles. Show a heatmap of the topics the resident has handled clinically and the ones they have only read about. Tapping a cold cell suggests reading, or 'ask to be rostered to X'. _Why:_ It turns a credentialling tally into a study map. Exam prep is strongest when tied to cases you remember, and gaps show early, not in R5.
- **Auto-generated viva questions from each case** (effort L, radical 4/5). After a case is saved (opt-in, can be done later), generate 2-3 viva-style questions from its summary and tags. Example: for 'OLV for VATS, desat to 85%', ask 'Outline your hypoxaemia algorithm during OLV' and 'Physiology of HPV'. Questions queue into the spaced-review deck; answers can be linked to a reflection or a note. _Why:_ It mirrors the oral exam format ('tell me about a case you did...') and teaches residents to pull principles out of routine work. Bolder versions use an LLM with on-device redaction.
- **Deliberate practice planner: 'next 4 weeks' goals** (effort M, radical 2/5). Turn Still needed and the progress gaps into a short deliberate-practice plan. The resident picks 1-3 specific skills (e.g. 'awake fibreoptic intubation', 'paeds caudal') with a measurable target and a feedback source (the supervisor's WBA in evals). The Case tab then quietly shows a 'goal' chip when a matching tag is picked, and a weekly check shows progress against each goal. _Why:_ Volume does not equal expertise. Deliberate practice needs explicit goals, feedback and reflection, and the app already knows the gaps and has the evals app on the same origin for feedback.
- **Pre-list prep cards (forward-looking retrieval)** (effort M, radical 3/5). 'Plan for later' and upcoming (future-dated) cases already exist. For a planned case, the night before, show a prep card: 'You have done 3 similar cases; here is what you wrote last time', plus 3 prompts to predict the key risks before reading. After the case, the summary prompts 'Did your prediction hold?' _Why:_ Prediction, then outcome, then comparison is a powerful learning loop (the generation effect and prediction error). It reuses the resident's own past cases as worked examples just before they are needed.
- **Interleaved case quiz across categories** (effort S, radical 2/5). A 10-question 'mixed bag' quiz built from the resident's own cases across different APMES tags: given an anonymised summary, guess the category, the EPA, or the likely complication. It is interleaved on purpose, so cardiac, obstetric and paeds cases come mixed together. _Why:_ Interleaving improves discrimination between problem types, which is the core skill in an exam case scenario. Category guessing also teaches residents the APMES taxonomy and so improves the quality of their tagging.
- **Reflection-to-principle extraction ('pearls')** (effort S, radical 2/5). Add an optional 'One pearl' line to reflections and to ☆ Interesting cases: a single takeaway sentence. Pearls become a searchable personal handbook and feed the spaced-review deck as cloze cards ('In OLV hypoxaemia, first ___'). _Why:_ Generative summarising aids encoding. A personal pearl library built from real cases outlasts residency and is quick to review before exams.
- **Cohort-shared, de-identified case bank for exam prep** (effort XL, radical 5/5). Residents can opt in to share a ☆ case, stripped of initials and dates and checked by a de-identification step, into a programme-wide question bank, tagged by syllabus topic. Faculty / PCs curate it and attach model answers. The bank then serves as a living, local SAQ/viva bank drawn from real cases at the residents' own hospitals. _Why:_ It turns hundreds of private logs into a shared teaching asset. Rare cases (e.g. MH, AFE) seen by one resident become learning for all.
- **Confidence calibration on each case** (effort S, radical 3/5). Add an optional 1-tap 'How confident were you running this?' (1-5) when saving a case, and later ask again during spaced review. Route `markdown.js` and the other innerHTML sinks through one sanitising policy. No CSP was found. _Why:_ Reflections, notes, shared templates and imported files all reach the renderer through Markdown. One XSS, from a malicious shared template written by a compromised admin or a crafted import file, would steal the Firebase session and with it every private reflection. A CSP is cheap protection in depth.
- **End-to-end encryption for reflections and notes** (effort XL, radical 5/5). Encrypt reflections, notepad and bin contents on the client with a key derived from a passphrase or a WebAuthn PRF passkey, so Firestore only ever holds ciphertext. Submissions to admins would be re-encrypted to a programme public key at submit time. _Why:_ The notes promise that reflections are private even from the app owner, but anyone with GCP console or Firebase plugin access can read them today. E2EE makes that promise true technically instead of relying on policy, which matters for candid writing about errors and wellbeing.
- **Recovery and key escrow designed in from the start** (effort L, radical 4/5). Pair E2EE or passkeys with recovery codes the resident prints or saves (shown once). Optionally add social recovery: a key split with Shamir shares between the resident, a PD and an offline owner share. Also add a 'lost Google account' process so a PD can re-link a resident record (`residents/{rid}.email`) to a new email, with a cooling-off period and an email to the old address. _Why:_ Identity today is the Google email. When a resident changes institution email or loses the account, `logbooks/{email}` is orphaned, and the fix is a manual console job by the owner with no safeguards against takeover through a PD editing `residents.email`.
- **Close the residents.email re-pointing takeover path** (effort S, radical 1/5). A PD can update `residents/{rid}` (validResident). `isMember()` and `isResident()` trust whichever email that record holds, so changing it hands someone else the right to write that resident's summary and upload images. Freeze `email` after creation unless an admin changes it, require a reason field, and notify both addresses. _Why:_ This is a concrete privilege-escalation path: one compromised PD account can impersonate a resident in the counts that drive 'who's behind'. It is cheap to fix in the rules and cheap to test in the emulator.
- **Lock down evaluation creation and assessor-private writes** (effort S, radical 1/5). Any signed-in user can create an evaluation naming themselves as resident and any assessorEmail. Require the creator to be `isMember()` and the assessor to be `isActiveFaculty()` or an admin. Rate-limit chasing with a `chasedAt` minimum interval. Check that `private/` writes cannot create orphan docs once status leaves 'requested'. _Why:_ Outsiders can currently spam faculty with assessment requests sent from the app's own domain, which makes this a phishing and harassment channel. Faculty trust in the evals flow is fragile.
- **Signed, verifiable portfolio exports** (effort L, radical 4/5). When a portfolio or summary is exported (docx/xlsx), add a hash of the case set and a server-side signature from a small Cloud Function or Cloudflare Worker holding a programme key. Add a public verify page where a PD drops the file in and sees whether it matches what was in the logbook on that date. _Why:_ Portfolios count towards progression decisions. Today a resident or anyone else can edit the exported docx afterwards, and an assessor cannot tell. Signatures make the logbook a trusted record instead of a convenience tool.
- **Security center and session control for residents** (effort M, radical 3/5). Add a More → Security page listing devices with recent sign-ins, the last export, the last submission and any privileged access to the logbook (from the audit trail). Include a 'sign out everywhere' button, which needs a small backend to revoke refresh tokens or a `sessionEpoch` field compared in the rules against `request.auth.token.auth_time`. _Why:_ Residents sign in on shared hospital PCs. Device recovery history and drafts stay in a browser until that browser signs out, so a forgotten session on a theatre PC leaks reflections. Residents need a way to kill a session remotely.
- **Short sessions on shared PCs with a re-auth gate for sensitive screens** (effort M, radical 3/5). Add a per-device 'shared computer' setting, off by default. It keeps auth in session persistence (`browserSessionPersistence`), auto-locks after idle time and requires a fresh Google re-auth (`auth_time` within 5 minutes, enforced in the rules for reads of reflections and notes) before showing reflections, notepad or exports. _Why:_ Reflections are the most sensitive data in the app and are often opened on ward or theatre workstations. A rules-enforced freshness check is stronger than a client-side PIN.
- **Continuous adversarial rules testing and a public threat model** (effort M, radical 2/5). Grow `tools/test-rules.sh` into a property-based fuzzer that generates random role combinations (resident, PD, admin, owner, faculty, stranger) and random write shapes against every path, and asserts invariants: no one but the owner reads reflections, a stranger cannot create more than X docs, and so on. Add a short threat-model section to NOTES.md listing each role's powers and run it in CI on every rules PR. _Why:_ The rules file is 577 lines of hand-written branches, deployed automatically on merge. Invariant tests catch regressions such as a widened `allow read` before the merge deploys them to production.

Add an optional one-tap 'confirm' link per case or per week that a consultant opens in demo-like read-only view (initials and tags only), so exported portfolios carry a 'supervisor-confirmed' count next to the self-logged count. _Why:_ Self-logged numbers are taken on trust today. Confirmed counts make the logbook usable as credentialling evidence rather than a personal diary, and this is the step ACGME and the RCoA systems have that this app lacks.
- **Streaks and 'log within 24 h' nudges (habit-app loop)** (effort M, radical 2/5). Borrow from Duolingo/Strava: a gentle per-device streak of 'days on list with cases logged', a weekly ring that fills toward the term's pace, and an optional evening reminder ('3 theatre days this week, 1 logged'). Off by default, like every new option here. _Why:_ NOTES says most residents only log for audit, so they batch-log late and lose detail. Habit loops are the cheapest proven fix for logging completeness.
- **Pace-to-target forecasting like ACGME minimums dashboards** (effort S, radical 1/5). Analytics already shows progress and 'Still needed'. Add a projection: at your current monthly rate per APMES tag, you will reach target by month X (or fall Y short at end of posting), with a red/amber/green per tag and 'cases to catch up per week'. _Why:_ Residents and PCs care about whether they will make the numbers in time, not only the total so far. A forecast turns the Still needed list into a plan.
- **Roster-aware logging prompts (link with nuh-roster)** (effort L, radical 4/5). The same site already runs the NUH daily roster. When the resident's roster says 'OT 5 list, Neuro', the Case tab preselects a matching tag group and asks 'log today's Neuro list?' at the end of the shift. Opt-in, per account. _Why:_ eLogbook and Kaizen are stand-alone; joining the roster and the logbook removes the 'what did I do today?' recall step and is something no competitor can easily copy.
- **Kaizen-style learning goals and PDP linked to tags** (effort M, radical 2/5). RCoA Lifelong Learning / Kaizen let trainees set goals ('10 awake fibreoptic intubations this term') that are ticked off by logged events. Let residents set personal goals on any APMES or personal tag with a date, shown beside the programme targets, and pulled into the Word portfolio as a PDP section. _Why:_ Moves the app from audit compliance toward learning, with almost no extra friction at logging time because goals ride on existing tags.
- **Voice or photo-of-list capture with on-device parsing** (effort XL, radical 4/5). Speak 'sixty-year-old male, lap chole, GA ETT, RSI' or snap the printed (de-identified) theatre list; on-device speech/OCR fills the case box and Paste-a-list review suggests tags. Images never leave the phone and are discarded after parsing. _Why:_ Typing on a phone between cases is the main friction; fitness apps won by making capture near-zero effort. The app already has list mode and suggest-as-you-type to build on.
- **Cohort benchmarking with anonymised percentiles** (effort M, radical 3/5). 'Compare with your intake' exists for totals; extend it to per-tag percentiles and case-mix shape (like Strava segments or ACGME national percentiles): 'your regional blocks are in the bottom quarter for PGY2'. Only aggregate counts, k-anonymity threshold before showing. _Why:_ Shows residents and PCs where exposure gaps are, which is the main thing national case-log reports are used for.
- **Faculty / PC cohort heat-map with early warning** (effort L, radical 3/5). For the Faculty / PCs profile, a grid of residents x APMES tags coloured by pace-to-target, with an automatic flag list ('4 residents unlikely to meet obstetric epidurals by end of posting') and one-click email of a nudge. _Why:_ ACGME programme directors live in these reports; giving PCs this turns the logbook into a programme-management tool and catches exposure gaps while there is time to fix the roster.
- **Spaced-repetition from your own interesting cases** (effort M, radical 4/5). Turn starred (Interesting) cases and reflections into flashcards: a weekly 'remember this case?' card asks what you would do differently, Anki-style intervals, answers saved as reflection follow-ups. _Why:_ No case logbook reuses its own data for learning. The star and reflections already exist; resurfacing them at spaced intervals is a proven way to retain lessons from real cases.
- **Year-in-review 'Wrapped' and shareable milestones** (effort M, radical 3/5). Spotify-Wrapped-style end-of-posting and end-of-year summary: total cases, top tags, firsts (first solo CSE, first paeds ETT), longest streak, a 'map' of your case-mix. Milestone cards ('100th neuraxial') shareable as images without case details. _Why:_ Fitness apps keep users through celebration. A positive recap makes logging feel worth it for residents who otherwise only log for audit.
- **Interoperable open export (FHIR / ACGME-like CSV) and portability** (effort M, radical 2/5). Offer a standard machine-readable export (a documented JSON/CSV schema mapped to procedure codes or SNOMED where possible) so a resident can carry their log to another programme, an overseas fellowship application, or the college's lifelong-learning record. _Why:_ eLogbook and ACGME logs are respected partly because they travel with the trainee. Portability raises the value of every case logged here.
- **AI case-summary coach for reflections** (effort L, radical 4/5). An optional assistant reads the de-identified case summary and tags and suggests (a) missing tags that count toward targets, (b) a reflection prompt tailored to the heading, (c) flags any text that looks identifying before save. _Why:_ Combines tag completeness, reflection quality and the privacy check in one step; competitors use AI mainly for search, not for nudging learning or safety.

### faculty

**Top pick:** Tap-to-confirm supervision with 10-second feedback: the resident tags the consultant when saving a case, and the consultant gets one card to confirm and add a feedback chip or one line. This one loop gives faculty teaching credit (the ledger), gives residents feedback while the case is fresh, makes the logbook's evidence verifiable, and feeds the growth timeline. No case details need to travel beyond the tags, date and the resident's initials.

- **Tap-to-confirm supervision ("I was there")** (effort L, radical 4/5). After saving a case, the resident can tag the supervising consultant. The consultant gets one push or email card: "Was with R2 for a difficult airway today? ✓ / 👍 good / 💬 one line." One tap confirms the case and tops up the consultant's teaching ledger. No case details travel, only the tags, date and the resident's initials. _Why:_ Consultants open apps that take under 10 seconds and give them something back. Verified supervision also makes the logbook more trustworthy for APMES audits.
- **Teaching credit ledger for faculty** (effort M, radical 3/5). A faculty-side page that totals the cases they supervised, the reflections they commented on and the evals they completed, broken down by tag and resident year. It exports as a CPD or teaching-portfolio statement for appraisal and promotion. _Why:_ Teaching is invisible work, and consultants need evidence of it for appraisal. If the app produces that evidence, faculty have a selfish reason to keep using it.
- **10-second feedback chips on a case** (effort M, radical 3/5). The consultant gets a feedback card with preset chips ("safe hands", "plan needed prompting", "ready for less supervision", "talk to me about X") and an optional voice note that is transcribed to one line. The feedback attaches to the resident's case and can turn into an evals/ workplace-based assessment (WBA) draft. _Why:_ Busy consultants won't write paragraphs. Preset chips get feedback written down while the case is still fresh, instead of weeks later on MedHub.
- **Growth timeline per trainee ("watch them grow")** (effort M, radical 3/5). The current #caselog shows one resident's counts against targets. Add a time axis: when they hit each target and their first case under each tag, with milestones like "first solo spinal" or "10th paediatric case". It is built from the counts summaries over time, never case details. _Why:_ Faculty stay engaged when they can see a trainee progress, not just tick boxes. It also lets a consultant spot a resident who has stalled.
- **Pre-list briefing: who's on my list today** (effort L, radical 5/5). The morning the consultant is rostered with a resident (link with nuh-roster), they get a card: "R1 with you today: has 2/10 obstetric GAs and no awake fibreoptic yet. Their stated goal: arterial lines." The resident writes that goal in the logbook beforehand. _Why:_ It turns an ordinary list into a planned teaching session with no extra admin. This is the moment when a consultant actually has the attention to spare.
- **Learning goal handshake** (effort M, radical 4/5). The resident sets a weekly or posting goal in the logbook ("lead 3 RSIs"). Supervising faculty can see it and press "I'll help". When a logged case matches the goal's tags, both sides see the goal marked done. _Why:_ It links what the resident wants to learn with the cases faculty can offer. The app becomes a two-way tool instead of a passive audit record.
- **Weekly faculty digest email or notification** (effort M, radical 2/5). A 30-second Monday digest. It covers which of your trainees are behind on which tags, who asked you for feedback, and a teaching-credit counter ("you supervised 14 cases logged last week"). It has one-tap deep links into #caselog. _Why:_ Consultants won't open a PWA by habit. A digest brings the app to them, and the Case logs data already exists.
- **Case-opportunity matchmaking** (effort L, radical 5/5). Faculty mark upcoming interesting lists or cases ("thoracic list Thursday, DLT likely"). Residents who are behind on that tag get a nudge and can claim the slot. It uses the existing 'Still needed' and 'behind' status. _Why:_ It turns the progress data into actual case exposure, gives faculty visible teaching wins, and fixes the gaps that the "who's behind" view only reports.
- **Reflection 'nod' and one-line comment** (effort M, radical 3/5). With the resident's consent, they share one reflection with a named consultant. The consultant can leave a nod or one line of comment, which counts as faculty engagement and appears in the Word portfolio export as a signed comment. _Why:_ Reflections are currently a solitary task. A light faculty touch makes them worth writing well, and it keeps privacy because the resident chooses what to share.
- **Trainee 'thank you' wall and teaching awards** (effort S, radical 3/5). Residents can send an anonymous thanks tied to a case's tag ("taught me the ultrasound-guided FICB"). The thanks pile up on the consultant's profile, and at year end they feed the programme's teaching awards automatically. _Why:_ Recognition is the cheapest motivator for faculty. It also gives PDs data on who actually teaches.
- **Entrustment slider instead of forms** (effort L, radical 4/5). For each tag cluster, faculty set one entrustment level per resident (1 observe → 5 could supervise others) with a single drag. These ratings build an entrustment heatmap across the programme, which can be mapped to the APMES EPAs in evals/. _Why:_ It matches how consultants actually think about trainees ("would I leave them alone?"). One gesture captures more than counts ever can.
- **Faculty home screen: three cards, nothing else** (effort S, radical 2/5). Redesign the Faculty / PCs profile's landing screen as three cards: (1) things waiting for me, such as confirmations, feedback requests and shared reflections; (2) my trainees this posting, each with a sparkline; (3) my teaching credit this month. Case logs and Admin stay one tap away. _Why:_ The current faculty view is built for PCs to check counts. A consultant with 90 seconds between cases needs only what's waiting for them and what's changed.
