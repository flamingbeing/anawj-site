## EPA 7: Providing anaesthetic care for obstetric patients

This EPA includes the following nested EPAs:

- a. Managing parturient for labour epidural/ combined spinal epidural (CSE)
- b. Managing parturient for Lower Segment Caesarean Section (LSCS)

**Specifications**

1. Assessing obstetric patients through history taking, physical examination and relevant investigations
2. Locating best available evidence and using it to formulate and justify anaesthetic plans for obstetric patients with consideration for common issues that impact obstetric anaesthesia care, complex issues in critically ill obstetric patients and patient preference
3. Anticipating potential obstetric, medical, or anaesthetic problems and formulating contingency plans
4. Providing patient consultation, counselling, and obtaining informed consent for procedures
5. Making appropriate referrals to other specialties
6. Communicating with patients and their families, and engaging them in shared decision making
7. Coordinating care of patients through effective communication between healthcare professionals in a multidisciplinary team to ensure safe transition of care and handovers especially in complex clinical situations like non obstetric surgery which requires extra perioperative precautions and monitoring for foetal wellbeing
8. Managing the difficult obstetric airway in accordance with the difficult obstetric airway algorithm
9. Performing central neuraxial regional anaesthesia (RA) in the obstetric patient
10. Managing complex obstetric conditions according to the latest guidelines such as severe pre-eclampsia, massive obstetric haemorrhage
11. Organising and managing resuscitation in obstetric emergencies such as eclampsia and massive obstetric haemorrhage, as part of a multidisciplinary team

**Limitations**

This EPA excludes the following:

1. Obstetric patients requiring intensive care
2. Medically complex mother-foetal dyad presenting for foetal surgery
3. Obstetric patients undergoing complicated non obstetric surgeries e.g., complicated oncological surgeries

**Sub-competencies:** PC1, PC2, PC3, PC4, PC5, PC7, PC8, PC10, MK1, MK2, SBP2, PBL1, PBL2, P1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

**Knowledge**

1. Physiological changes in pregnancy including haemodynamic consequences of pregnancy, labour, and delivery
2. Foetal/neonatal/ placental physiology
3. Applied pharmacology relevant to obstetric patients including local anaesthetics, agents affecting uterine tone, opioids and drug interactions, and potential effects on foetus including teratogenic effects
4. Applied anatomy relevant to regional anaesthesia for obstetrics including central neuraxial blocks and pain pathways involved in the first and second stages of labour
5. Pathophysiology of various common complications of pregnancy including gestational diabetes and pre-eclampsia
6. Anaesthetic implications and obstetric management of the following high-risk pregnancy conditions
   - a. Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy – preeclampsia, eclampsia
   - b. Multiple gestation
   - c. Preterm labour including use of tocolytics, dexamethasone
   - d. Abnormal foetal presentations
   - e. Peripartum haemorrhage
   - f. Maternal or foetal infection – HIV, sepsis, chorioamnionitis
   - g. Endocrine disease – diabetes mellitus, thyroid disease
   - h. Substance abuse
   - i. Respiratory diseases – asthma
   - j. Cardiovascular diseases – cardiomyopathy, congenital heart disease, valvular heart disease
   - k. Haematological or thromboembolic diseases – anaemia’s, idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura, von Willebrand’s disease
   - l. Morbid obesity
   - m. Musculoskeletal disorders – scoliosis, prior back surgery
7. Methods of providing analgesia/ anaesthesia for routine labour and vaginal delivery, and instrumental delivery such as vacuum extraction and trial of forceps (including drug dosages)
8. Methods of providing anaesthesia for elective or emergency surgical delivery such as use of existing labour epidural, spinal anaesthesia, and general anaesthesia (GA) (including drug dosages)
9. Methods of providing analgesia/ anaesthesia for Examination under anaesthesia (EUA) for postpartum haemorrhage, manual removal of placenta and tubal ligation
10. Anaesthetic considerations for the mother, foetus, and medically complex neonate
11. Resuscitation/ Trauma management of the pregnant patient
12. Resuscitation of the new-born including APGAR scoring, umbilical cord blood gas measurements

**Skills**

1. Clinical assessment skills
   - History taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations
2. Procedural skills
   - RA including spinal, epidural, and combined spinal epidural (CSE)
   - Obstetric airway skills
3. Communication and interpersonal skills
   - Patient-centric communication including counselling and taking informed consent from anxious patients, patients in labour or teenage mothers, and comforting patients and family in the setting of poor foetal outcome/demise
   - Healthcare professional/interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of obstetric patients including team brief/huddling and accurate documentation
4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management)

**Attitudes**

1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
4. Provides efficient patient-centric compassionate care in a timely manner while ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

**Experiences**

1. Clinical rotations in obstetrics in senior and junior residency: minimum 4 months in Junior Residency (R1 to R3), minimum 1 month in Senior Residency (R4 to R5)
2. Cases logs
3. Interprofessional team training workshop/simulation including maternal and neonatal resuscitation

**Potential risks in case of failure**

1. Increase in maternal morbidity/ dissatisfaction/mortality
2. Increase in adverse foetal/neonatal outcomes
3. Increase in medico-legal liability
4. Increase healthcare cost

## EPA 7a: Managing parturient for labour epidural/ combined spinal epidural (CSE)

3. Formulating and implementing an appropriate labour epidural analgesia plan
4. Performing central neuraxial anaesthesia including using ultrasound to identify interspinous spaces and the midline in patients with complex neuraxial anatomy
5. Managing failed/ inadequate labour epidural analgesia and complications related to labour epidural/ CSE such as Post Dural Puncture Headache (PDPH), hypotension, backache, dural puncture, foetal bradycardia after initiation of epidural/ CSE

## EPA 7b: Managing parturient for Lower Segment Caesarean Section (LSCS)

**Specifications**

1. Assessing patient through history taking, physical examination and relevant investigations
2. Identifying and escalating care of high-risk parturient presenting for LSCS
3. Formulating and implementing an appropriate anaesthetic plan for patients presenting for LSCS
4. Counselling patients for anaesthesia for caesarean section by:
   - Eliciting patient’s values, goals, and preferences
   - Discussing risks, benefits of anaesthesia and/or alternatives.
5. Managing inadequate/failed spinal anaesthetic and its complications such as high block, hypotension, and neurological deficits
6. Managing high risk obstetric patients requiring lower segment caesarean section, such as those with preeclampsia, using a multidisciplinary approach
7. Recognising crisis situations such as malignant hyperthermia or local anaesthetic systemic toxicity, calling for help early and initiating management
8. Anticipating and managing difficult obstetric airway using standard obstetric airway algorithm
9. Coordinating care of patients in clinical situations utilising the roles of the interprofessional teams
10. Performing safe and effective transition of care/handovers in routine clinical situations.
11. Managing complex or stressful situations such as massive peripartum haemorrhage, complex ethical situations

## EPA 8: Providing anaesthetic care for patients in a non-operating theatre setting

**Specifications**

Remote locations for procedures that require Anaesthesia care may include the following settings:

- Diagnostic Radiology and Intervention Radiology Centre
- Cardiac Catheterisation Laboratory
- Radiotherapy Centre
- MRI Suite
- Endoscopy Centre
- Ambulatory Surgical Centre
- Burns Centre
- Dental Centre

Patients who require anaesthesia care in remote locations can range from well patients who need sedation to tolerate a procedure, to patients who require anaesthesia support because they are in a higher risk category (e.g., endovascular clot retrieval for stroke).

1. Evaluating suitability of patients for procedures in remote locations
2. Formulating and executing anaesthesia plans for patients undergoing procedures and interventions in remote locations
3. Coordinating and communicating between multidisciplinary professionals to facilitate patient care

**Limitations**

This EPA excludes the following:

1. Procedures involving the heart and/or access to heart chambers

**Sub-competencies:** PC1, PC2, PC3, PC4, PC5, PC7, PC8, MK1, MK2, SBP2, PBL1, P1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

**Knowledge**

1. **Diseases or conditions that require interventional procedures in remote locations**
   - Liver diseases (intrahepatic cysts) and portal hypertension (requiring transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt, percutaneous radiofrequency ablation therapy)
   - Paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia (for radiofrequency catheter ablation)
   - Cerebral aneurysm (for endovascular intervention)
   - Brain tumour (for radiotherapy)
   - Biliary duct disease: Choledocholithiasis, biliary duct stenosis (for endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography and interventions)
   - Renal cyst
   - Burns injury
2. **Interventional procedure specifics including procedure related requirements and complications**
   - Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt for portal hypertension
   - Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography and interventions
   - Radiofrequency catheter liver cysts, renal cysts
   - Cardioversion
   - Cerebral endovascular interventions (hemodynamic targets)

   - Radiocontrast media (barium-based (orally); iodinated agents and gadolinium contrast media (intravenously)
3. **Specific features/ hazards of remote locations**
   - Diagnostic Radiology and Intervention Radiology Centres, Cardiac Catheterisation Laboratories, Radiotherapy Centres
   - Magnetic resonance imaging suites
   - Endoscopy Centres
   - Ambulatory Surgical Centres
   - Burns Centres
   - Dental Centres
4. **Anaesthesia considerations for procedures in remote locations**
   - i. **Working space**
     - Central gas supplies
     - Equipment available for monitoring, drug delivery, oxygen delivery, anaesthesia delivery warming fluid, resuscitation, airway management

     - Radiation safety
     - Safety guidelines (in magnetic resonance suite, for procedures requiring image intensification, and radiotherapy)

     - Dental chair anaesthesia

   - Intravascular access (venous/ arterial/ Vascath)
   - Insertion of devices within the nasal, oropharyngeal, oesophageal, bladder and gastric cavity for the purpose of monitoring, extraction of gases or bodily fluids and/or infusion of gases or fluids
3. Communication and interpersonal skills
   - Patient/ family centric communication including breaking bad news, end of life discussions

## EPA 12: Assessing and managing patients with chronic pain

**Specifications**

1. Performing assessment with focused history-taking, physical examination, and relevant investigations to ascertain the following:
   - i. Biological basis for pain symptoms
   - ii. Psychological state of the patient
   - iii. Social impact of pain such as ability to function, rest and pursue interests and goals
2. Diagnosing common chronic pain conditions such as spinal pain, neuropathy
3. Formulating patient-centric, multimodal, multidisciplinary management chronic pain management plans using the biopsychosocial model approach
4. Consulting pain specialist or allied health care professionals appropriately
5. Communicating with patients and their families to allay fears and manage expectations, make informed decisions regarding their chronic pain condition, and counsel regarding treatment strategies including potential side effects of medications or complications of pain treatment procedures

### How are WBAs linked to EPAs?

EPAs operationalise competencies into actual practice by placing focus on the performance of actual tasks. WBAs allow a direct assessment of the performance of these tasks, and in turn allow (i) identification of resident competency gaps; and (ii) a decision to be made on residents’ level of readiness to perform the task. Entrustment decisions about EPAs require longitudinal mentorship, and having sufficient supervision and feedback is key to good entrustment decisions. ([author names omitted], 2016)

> WBAs are formative and provide evidence to support entrustment of EPAs!

- **To support entrustment decisions on EPAs**

With WBAs, individual supervisors can make a decision on the level of entrustment to perform the task at that point in time (‘formative decision’) or during the period where the resident is under their supervision. For a summative entrustment decision to be made, the Clinical Competency Committee (CCC) should review multiple WBAs completed by multiple assessors at multiple timepoints, to gain a confident and holistic view of that the resident is able to prospectively perform at that level of entrustment. WBAs, therefore, provide critical sources of information on the resident’s competency level to support and inform entrustment decisions. The minimum number of WBAs that must be reviewed will be included in the respective EPA templates as Sources of Information.
