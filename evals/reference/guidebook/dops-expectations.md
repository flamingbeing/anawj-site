# Guideline & Expectation for DOPS

Attachment to the Anaesthesiology EPA Guidebook (v8). For each procedure: the Pre-procedure / Procedure / Post-procedure checklist, followed by the expectations for each DOPS item (Below / Meeting / Above Expectations). Line breaks within table cells are shown as `<br>`; numbering of checklist items follows the source.

## DOPS for Endotracheal Intubation (Direct Laryngoscopy)

### Pre-Procedure

1. Checks correct indication for endotracheal intubation
2. Performs airway and dental assessment
3. Takes informed consent
4. Chooses appropriate type and size of endotracheal tube (ETT)
5. Checks ETT preparation- ensures cuff checked and ETT lubricated
6. Checks ancillary equipment e.g., anaesthesia machine, laryngoscope, circuit, oral/ nasopharyngeal airways, tapes, syringe, Magill’s forceps, suction

### Procedure

1. Nonsterile gloves
2. Positions patient comfortably to achieve horizontal alignment of the external auditory meatus and sternal notch
3. Attains sniffing the morning air position
4. Preoxygenates patient
5. Mask-holds and assists ventilation appropriately- triple maneuver when patient sedated and relaxed (head tilt, chin lift, jaw thrust)
6. Checks airway patency and adequacy of ventilation, including capnography, using adjuncts like oral airway as necessary
7. Laryngoscope blade insertion with left hand into right side of mouth
8. Lifts blades and avoids pivoting on upper incisors
9. Identifies vocal cords
10. Introduces ETT from right side of mouth and insert past cords to appropriate depth
11. Removes laryngoscope
12. Removes gloves
13. Inflates cuff
14. Connects breathing circuit
15. Confirms ETT correctly positioned in trachea (5-point auscultation and capnography)
16. Secures ETT position with use of tape/ ties as appropriate

### Post-Procedure

1. Checks cuff pressure
2. Sets appropriate ventilation settings on anaesthesia machine e.g., TV 6-8 ml/kg, PEEP of 4-5
3. Documents

## DOPS for Difficult Intubation

### Pre-procedure

1. Checks correct indication for endotracheal intubation
2. Performs airway and dental assessment and recognizes potential difficult intubation
3. Takes informed consent
4. Chooses appropriate type and size of endotracheal tube (ETT)
5. Checks ETT preparation- ensures cuff checked and ETT lubricated
6. Chooses and checks appropriate videolaryngoscope
7. Checks ancillary equipment e.g., anaesthesia machine, breathing circuit, oral/ nasopharyngeal airways, gum elastic bougie, stylet, tapes, syringe, Magill’s forceps, suction

### Procedure

1. Nonsterile gloves
2. Positions patient comfortably to achieve horizontal alignment of the external auditory meatus and sternal notch
3. Attains sniffing the morning air position
4. Preoxygenates patient
5. Mask-holds and assists ventilation appropriately- triple maneuver when patient sedated and relaxed (head tilt, chin lift, jaw thrust)
6. Checks airway patency and adequacy of ventilation, including capnography, using adjuncts like oral airway as necessary
7. Laryngoscope blade insertion with left hand into right side of mouth
8. Looks at screen and optimizes laryngeal view
9. Looks at oral cavity and introduces ETT/ aids like gum elastic bougie as appropriate
10. Looks at screen, passes ETT through vocal cords, and notes marking on the ETT at the level of the vocal cords
11. Removes videolaryngoscope
12. Removes gloves
13. Inflates cuff
14. Connects breathing circuit
15. Confirms ETT correctly positioned in trachea (5-point auscultation and capnography)
16. Secures ETT position with use of tape/ ties as appropriate

### Post Procedure

1. Checks cuff pressure
2. Puts patient on appropriate ventilation mode
3. Documents

## DOPS for Double Lumen Tube (DLT)

### Pre- Procedure

1. Checks correct indication for double lumen tube (DLT) intubation
2. Performs airway and dental assessment and recognizes potential difficult intubation
3. Takes informed consent
4. Chooses appropriate type and size of DLT
5. Checks DLT preparation- ensure cuff checked and DLT lubricated
6. Checks ancillary equipment e.g., anaesthesia machine, breathing circuit, laryngoscope, oral/ nasopharyngeal airways, gum elastic bougie, stylet, tapes, bronchoscope, syringe, suction

### Procedure

1. Sterile gloves for handling FOB/ non-sterile gloves for general airway management
2. Positions patient comfortably supine in sniff the morning air position
3. Preoxygenates patient and induces anaesthesia with use of muscle relaxant
4. Mask-holds and assists ventilation appropriately- triple maneuver when sedated and relaxed (head tilt, chin lift, jaw thrust)
5. Checks airway patency and adequacy of ventilation, including capnography using adjuncts like oral airway as necessary
6. Performs laryngoscopy and introduces DLT from right side of mouth with bronchial concave curve facing anteriorly
7. As tip passes through larynx, rotates the tracheal tube anti-clockwise 90 degrees for left DLT or clockwise 90 degrees for right DLT until resistance met and inflates cuff of tracheal tube
8. Removes laryngoscope
9. Removes non-sterile gloves
10. Connects breathing circuit
11. Confirms ETT correctly positioned in trachea (5-point auscultation and capnography)
12. Secures DLT
13. Puts on sterile gloves
14. Inserts bronchoscope via tracheal tube to confirm correct placement with bronchial cuff visible just below the carina in the intended bronchus
15. Inserts bronchoscope via bronchial tube as appropriate to confirm position particularly if a right sided DLT was inserted.
16. Adjusts DLT depth as appropriate and re-secure if done
17. Puts on machine ventilation
18. If patient is turned lateral, checks with bronchoscope, and confirms correct placement
19. To induce one lung ventilation (OLV), inflates the bronchial cuff, clamps connector of the intended side of lung to be collapsed and confirms clinically through auscultation

### Post-Procedure

1. Checks tracheal cuff pressure
2. Puts patient on appropriate ventilation mode
3. Documents

## DOPS for Laryngeal Mask Airway (LMA) Insertion

### Pre-procedure

1. Checks correct indication for LMA use
2. Excludes contraindications
3. Performs appropriate airway and dental assessment
4. Takes informed consent
5. Chooses appropriate type and size of LMA
6. Checks LMA preparation-ensure cuff checked and LMA lubricated
7. Checks ancillary equipment e.g., anaesthesia machine, breathing circuit, oral/ nasopharyngeal airways, tapes, syringe, suction

### Procedure

1. Nonsterile gloves
2. Positions patient comfortably
3. Preoxygenates patient
4. Checks adequate plane of anaesthesia
5. Holds LMA with properly-Pencil grip
6. Extends head and flex neck with care
7. Presses tip of cuff against hard palate and flattens it
8. Uses index finger to guide LMA through to hypopharynx
9. Removes gloves and connects circuit
10. Inflates cuff and observes slight ‘pop-up’ of LMA
11. Checks position and confirms adequate ventilation
12. Secure LMA with tapes

### Post Procedure

1. Checks cuff pressure
2. Puts patient on appropriate ventilation mode
3. Documents

## DOPS for Peripheral Nerve Block (PNB)

### Pre-procedure

1. Reviews indication and contraindications
2. Reviews blood results
3. Obtains informed consent
4. Applies monitors
5. Ensures IV in situ
6. Positions patient
7. Selects ultrasound probe
8. Performs pre-procedure ultrasound scan
9. Prepares local anaesthetics (+ adjuncts)
10. Selects needle

### Procedure

1. Observes aseptic precautions
2. Prepares ultrasound probe
3. Maintains communication with patient
4. Performs block time out
5. Administers subcutaneous local anaesthetic
6. Visualises needle and sonoanatomy
7. Performs Intermittent aspiration
8. Avoids paraesthesia with repositioning of needle if encountered
9. Administers local anaesthetic (+ adjuncts) in aliquots
10. Manages puncture site

### Post Procedure

1. Ensures proper disposal of sharps
2. Assesses block efficacy
3. Documents

## DOPS for Labour Combined Spinal Epidural/ Epidural Anaesthesia

### Pre-Procedure

1. Ensures no contraindications for epidural including clinical risk of bleeding\*,
2. Reviews blood results
3. Obtains informed consent
4. Applies appropriate monitoring and including CTG and ensures CTG stable with clearance from obstetrician to proceed with epidural
5. Ensures IV in-situ
6. Preloads patient with intravenous fluid as appropriate
7. Positions patient
8. Site inspection and marking of level
9. Ensures LA (+adjuncts) and epidural/ CSE set available

\*OAA/AAGBI/RA UK guidelines for low platelets

Platelet count should be checked before any neuraxial procedure if there is any suspicion of decreasing platelet numbers during routine antenatal testing, signs of the development of pre-eclampsia, e.g., proteinuria or hypertension, or other clinical features suggesting coagulopathy, placental abruption or if the patient has been given recent anticoagulant therapy. Platelet numbers can decrease in patients treated with regular heparin for > 4 days. Otherwise, it would not be routine to check platelet numbers. and delay neuraxial block whilst these results are awaited.

### Procedure

1. Observes aseptic precautions, gown, mask, cap and sterile gloves
2. Prepares sterile trolley and draw drugs
3. Ensures ergonomics
4. Communicates with patient throughout procedure
5. Cleans with antiseptic solution and drape
6. Infiltrates skin with local anaesthetic
7. Uncaps epidural Touhy needle and spinal needle
8. Inserts Touhy needle to appropriate depth until engaged in ligament
9. Directs Touhy needle with bevel and needle curve facing cranially
10. Gradually advances Touhy needle in a stable manner, with constant pressure on loss of resistance (LOR) syringe and appreciation of tissue planes
11. Achieves LOR without dural puncture
12. Is aware of steps to take in event of inadvertent dural puncture with Touhy needle
13. \*Inserts spinal needle with docking complex in a stable manner
14. \*Observes adequate CSF backflow
15. \*Awareness of steps to take in event of paraesthesia or lack of CSF backflow
16. \*Fixes drug- filled syringe to spinal needle in a stable manner
17. \*Slow injection of appropriate dose of spinal LA (+ adjuncts) without paraesthesia
18. \*Removes spinal needle with docking complex and syringe
19. Inserts epidural catheter to appropriate depth
20. Is aware of steps to take in event unable to advance epidural catheter
21. Removes Touhy needle while keeping epidural catheter in place in a stable manner
22. Adjusts epidural catheter to appropriate depth
23. Ensures negative aspiration of CSF or Blood from crocodile clip
24. Is aware of steps to take in event of CSF/ blood aspiration in catheter
25. Administers test dose of 3mls 1% lignocaine given through filter
26. Secures epidural catheter in place with low risk of slipping or kinking

\*Omit if for epidural only

### Post Procedure

1. Ensures proper disposal of sharps
2. Assesses level of anaesthesia block
3. Reviews vital signs
4. Documents

## DOPS for Dialysis Vascular Access Catheter (Internal Jugular Vein)

### Pre-procedure

1. Ensures correct indication
2. Excludes contraindication
3. Reviews blood results
4. Obtains informed consent
5. Ensures appropriate monitoring
6. Justifies site selection
7. Pre procedure ultrasound scan
8. Ensures appropriate length of dialysis catheter selected
9. Provides appropriate sedation as required
10. Provides oxygen supplementation as appropriate

### Procedure

1. Positions patient comfortably and appropriately depending on site for insertion
2. Observes aseptic precautions: mask, gown, sterile glove, cap
3. Checks and prepares equipment including appropriate dialysis catheter on sterile trolley
4. Flushes and locks the lumen of dialysis catheter
5. Prepares ultrasound probe with sterile covering
7. Cleans with antiseptic solution and drapes patient
8. Infiltrates skin with local anaesthesia
9. Places patient in Trendelenburg position for neckline
10. Landmarks palpation/ visualization with ultrasound of central vein if doing real time
11. Inserts needle followed by guidewire to appropriate length (Seldinger’s technique)
12. Checks guidewire position is in vein and not artery with ultrasound or transducing pressure in the vessel before inserting dilator
13. Dilates track and railroads dialysis catheter to suitable length
14. Removes guidewire (call out advocated)
15. Checks backflow from all ports and heparin locks all ports
16. Secures catheter with sutures (3 anchoring points) and applies dressing
17. Ensures all hubs capped before removal of drapes

*(Numbering as in source: there is no item 6.)*

### Post-procedure

1. Ensures proper disposal of sharps
2. Orders Chest X-Ray (CXR) for neck lines
3. Documents accurately and completely

## DOPS for Scope Assisted Tracheal Intubation

*Note: in the source the expectations table for this procedure is headed “Expectations: DOPS for Awake Fibreoptic Intubation”.*

### Pre-Procedure

1. Check correct indication for awake flexible scope assisted tracheal intubation
2. Excludes relative/absolute contraindications e.g., barrier to patient cooperation, hypoxia requiring immediate intubation, blood or significant secretions in airway, severely narrowed airway with risk of ‘cork-in-bottle’ phenomenon, friable tissue with high risk of contact bleeding
3. Obtains informed consent
4. Performs airway and dental assessment
5. Checks and prepares scope equipment e.g., white balance and anti-fog, and local anaesthetic for topicalization before commencing procedure
6. Chooses appropriate type and size of endotracheal tube (ETT) and checks ETT preparation-ensures cuff checked and ETT lubricated and loads the ETT onto the flexible scope
7. Positions patient and scope monitor, airway trolley, suction
8. Provides patient with supplemental oxygen
9. Ensures IV cannula in place
10. Ensure monitoring in place especially pulse oximeter
11. Provides appropriate sedation as required
12. Checks ancillary equipment e.g., anaesthesia machine, laryngoscope, circuit, oral/ nasopharyngeal airways, tapes, syringe, Magill’s forceps, suction

### Procedure

1. Sterile gloves
2. Ensures adequate local anaesthetic topicalization to nose/ oropharynx/ larynx, with attention to maximum allowable dose of LA
3. Administers topical vasoconstrictor to nasal mucosa if applicable
4. Considers appropriate use of anti-sialogogue
5. Holds flexible scope and manipulates tip correctly
6. Advances scope through the nose or mouth
7. Identifies pharyngeal anatomy/ larynx correctly
8. Inserts scope through the larynx to just above the carina
9. Railroads the endotracheal tube (ETT) over the scope into the trachea
10. Confirms tip of ETT within trachea above carina and notes marking at nose/lip
11. Removes the scope
12. Secures ETT with tape
13. Removes gloves
14. Connects ETT to breathing circuit
15. Confirms correct tracheal tube position with auscultation of chest and end tidal capnography

### Post-procedure

1. Checks cuff pressure
2. Induces anaesthesia (if required)
3. Puts patient on appropriate ventilation mode
4. Documents
