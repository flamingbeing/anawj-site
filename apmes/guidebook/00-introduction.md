## How to use this guidebook

This guidebook is intended to inform both residents and faculty on Entrustable Professional Activities (EPAs) which will be implemented from July 2023.

All 3 anaesthesiology residency programmes have agreed upon the content of the EPAs and the minimum assessments that are stated under each EPA. The EPAs for anaesthesiology have also been approved by the Specialist Accreditation Board (SAB) as of 30 November 2022. Each residency programme however retains the right to variation in the implementation of the EPAs, for example by deciding if the levels of supervision are to be attained ahead of the timeline stipulated in the EPA or if the minimum number of Workplace-Based Assessments (WBAs) required to satisfy institutional accreditation is above the number stipulated in the EPAs.

Both residents and faculty are advised to familiarise themselves with the EPAs and the required assessments that come with it.

Since the start of residency, we have moved towards a Competency-based Medical Education (CBME) system that articulates the standard level of proficiency for all graduates, aligned with the roles graduates will need to fulfil in their careers.

Faculty will be familiar with WBAs such as the mini-Clinical Examination (mini-CEX) and Direct Observation of Procedural Skills (DOPS). For EPAs, WBAs have been modified to include entrustment questions so that the competency level of a trainee can be assessed not only from observation of the task or activity performed by the trainee, but also from the trainee’s answers to questions that go beyond the scope of the task at hand i.e., entrustment questions.

The WBA forms that will be used in EPAs are found under the section on EPA assessment forms (refer to page 7).

The EPA assessment forms have different descriptors for the Likert scores from those used in the current WBA forms, that faculty may be familiar with. Both faculty and residents should read the section on EPA assessment forms for details.

Each component of the workplace-based assessment has also been mapped to sub-competencies stipulated in the milestones to aid the Clinical Competency Committee (CCC) in their assessment of the resident’s progress and development.

You may find examples of entrustment questions that can be asked for each WBA under the entrustment questions section of each EPA. It is not necessary to ask all the entrustment questions suggested but at least 2 entrustment questions should be asked per WBA. The faculty has the prerogative to come up with his or her own entrustment question as long as it allows the faculty to discern and be able to decide on an appropriate level of supervision.

Workplace-Based Assessments are formative in nature and conducted so that the trainee can learn through and from the assessment. It is crucial that the EPA forms are filled soon after the assessment is completed, and feedback is provided. All forms should be filled within 24 hours of the assessment that is triggered by the resident to the faculty, failing which a reminder email will be sent to the faculty. A brief section on providing feedback provides basic principles and models that can be used by faculty as guides.

The words resident and trainee are used interchangeably.

## Introduction to Entrustable Professional Activities (EPA)

EPAs are defined as the core professional activities (work or tasks) that define a profession. For the Anaesthesiology specialty, 12 EPAs have been identified to be assessed during the residency training through Workplace-Based Assessments (WBA). Each EPA is a unit of professional practice (work or task) that may be entrusted to a learner to execute unsupervised once he or she has demonstrated the required competence.

Features of a trainee that enable trust include capability, integrity, reliability, humility, and agency. Capability encompasses the necessary knowledge, procedural and organisational skills, and clinical judgement to perform the professional task well. Integrity implies honesty and professionalism in keeping patient’s welfare paramount even if it means admitting to errors or oversight and not withholding information. Reliable trainees are recognised for their sense of responsibility, conscientiousness, dependability in following through with tasks and the accuracy of information they provide. Humility is the ability to know one’s limits in terms of knowledge and skills and be willing to ask for help in a timely and appropriate manner. It is also shown by the willingness to seek, receive and act on feedback. Agency refers to a trainee taking initiative and being proactive, whether in relation to patient care or personal professional development.

We recognise that EPAs cannot assess everything that a trainee must know to do, for example crisis management, and therefore serve as part of the programmatic assessment a trainee must complete.

To help faculty make an assessment and decision to trust, faculty need to observe the resident’s performance, discuss with the resident, and review what has been done by the resident such as documentation in the electronic health records, case logs and reflection pieces.

For a resident to successfully complete the residency training and be recognised as a specialist, in addition to fulfilling the necessary clinical and cognitive experience and assessments such as the Master of Medicine in Anaesthesiology examinations and case log numbers, the stipulated level of entrustment must be attained for all 12 EPAs as shown in Table 1.

A description of the progressive levels of entrustments and the corresponding level of supervision is provided in Table 2.

### Table 2: Description of levels of entrustment and corresponding levels of supervision

| Level | Supervision | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 | Observe only | The trainee has permission only to be present but not to enact this EPA. |
| Level 2 | Direct supervision | Supervisor needs to be present in the SAME room as the resident at all times. Pro-active supervision is required for all / most clinical activities. |
| Level 3 | Indirect supervision | Supervisor need not be in the same room as resident but remains within the healthcare facility, contactable and ready to respond within minutes to any call for help by the resident e.g., the supervisor having a meal break while the resident remains alone to care for the patient |
| Level 4 | Distant supervision | Supervisor is not present in healthcare facility but remains contactable via phone call to provide advice and supervision remotely. |
| Level 5 | Supervise | The trainee is able to guide others in this EPA. |

Residents will exit the residency training at level 4 entrustment in all EPAs with the exception of EPA 10 and 12 at level 3 entrustment because these require additional fellowship training to attain level 4 entrustment.

### The structure of an EPA

Each EPA has

1. A title that states the EPA.
2. Specifications and limitations that describe the EPA and qualify what is included in this EPA.
3. Potential risk in case of failure which states the risks to patient and healthcare system if the EPA is practised poorly.
4. Most relevant domains of sub-competency that describe patient care, medical knowledge, interpersonal communication skills, practice-based learning, professionalism, or systems-based practice competencies required of the trainee for successful practice of the EPA.
5. Knowledge, skills, attitudes, and experiences section that states stipulated specifics of these required for each EPA.
6. Sources of information which include case log numbers, written reflections, assessments and course/ workshop certification or attendance, provide documented evidence of experiential learning in the EPA.
7. Level of entrustment that stipulates the timeline for attainment of the expected level of entrustment and supervision. This allows the CCC to track the progression of the residents and allow for early interventions to ensure the resident has the necessary exposure, experience, and knowledge in order to attain the expected level of entrustment by the expected timeline. If the trainees do not meet the expectations, it is likely that CCC will not sanction the progression of the trainee across residency years.
8. Expiration date for the EPA is standardised at 1 year across all EPAs for Anaesthesiology. This means that if the trainee is absent from clinical training for more than a year, he/she will assume a lower level of entrustment and supervision upon resumption of clinical training. The trainee will be able to resume the EPA at a higher level of entrustment and supervision after completing the necessary assessments.

A full description of the EPA serves as a guide for both trainee and supervisors to know what the individual is ready and permitted to do within the healthcare system.

## EPA Assessment Forms

If there is any conflict of interest with regards to assessing the resident, faculty should highlight to the residency programme. Residents should also not choose assessors who may be conflicted in their assessment of the resident e.g., close relations.

The EPA assessment forms used are:

- a. Mini-Clinical Examination (Mini-CEX) Form *(attachment: miniCEX.pdf)*
- b. Direct Observation of Procedural Skills (DOPS) Form *(attachment: DOPS.pdf)*
- c. Entrustment-based Discussion (EBD) Form *(attachment: EbD.pdf)*
- d. Competency Evaluation Form *(attachment: Competency Form.pdf)*
- e. Multisource 360° Feedback (MSF) Form *(attachment: 360Feedback.pdf)*

The EPA assessment forms will be administered via MedHub; all faculty and residents should have a MedHub account (details on access and use are in the attached MedHub Quick Start Guide).

The resident is expected to take ownership and ensure that all relevant mini-CEX, DOPS, and EBD for an EPA are completed by the end of each clinical rotation. Programmes will provide residents with instructions for MSF 360 evaluation forms. Programmes will also match faculty for the competency evaluation form. Only physician faulty/ core faculty may be assessors for mini-CEX, DOPS and EBD. Senior residents will not be involved with the assessment of EPAs.

During the WBA, the assessor should decide if the resident has been observed to:

1. Be proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback.
2. Be honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible.
3. Recognise own limits and escalate to supervisor when appropriate.
4. Provide efficient patient-centric compassionate care in a timely manner while ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality.
5. Be a collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others.

### Mini-CEX/ DOPS/ EBD forms

These forms are specific to the EPA and are available for selection in MedHub. Both faculty and resident must agree on the case for assessment. After the observed WBA, the resident must trigger the EPA form by matching it to the assessor. The case specifics should be filled in by the resident before the faculty fills up the form. The same patient can be used for at most 2 EPA assessments as agreed with the faculty, provided all the relevant specific forms are filled up separately e.g., the same patient with respiratory disease can be used for EPA 1 mini-CEX for preoperative assessment of a patient in the hospital setting e.g. preoperative clinic/ pre-anaesthetic evaluation clinic (PEC), and EBD for respiratory disease, as long as both the mini-CEX form for preoperative assessment form and the EBD form for assessment of a patient with pre-existing respiratory disease are completed.

For mini-CEX and DOPS, the assessment is done by faculty observing the resident’s performance with minimal interference unless the patient’s well-being and safety are at risk.

The scoring system for the observation portion of the mini-CEX and DOPS are the same. These are based on a 9-point Likert scale with the following descriptors:

1. Trainee cannot perform this activity.
2. The trainee can perform this activity only under direct supervision with supervisor performing more than 50% of activity.
3. The trainee can perform this activity only under direct supervision with supervisor performing less than 50% of activity.
4. The trainee can perform this activity but requires occasional direct supervision.
5. The trainee can perform this activity under indirect supervision for uncomplicated case.
6. The trainee can perform this activity under indirect supervision for complicated case.
7. The trainee can perform this activity independently with distant supervision.
8. The trainee performs this activity independently with distant supervision well enough to supervise others.
9. The trainee performs this activity independently with distant supervision well enough to instruct others.

This scoring system is different from the current Likert scale that is based on the faculty’s expectation of the resident’s performance as compared to his/ her cohort of the same year. In this new system, for each subcomponent activity of the mini-CEX e.g., patient assessment/ preparation or preparation of physical location, the faculty is expected to assess the level of supervision required based on the observed performance.

If a subcomponent activity is not observed, please choose the option of not observed.

For the trainee to attain level 3 entrustment, a minimum score of 5 for overall clinical care or assessment is required for indirect supervision. For DOPS, this would correlate with satisfactory or meeting expectation.

For the trainee to attain level 4 entrustment, a minimum score of 7 for overall clinical care or assessment is required that would correlate with distant supervision.

For EBD, the assessment is done by faculty who has co-managed the case with the resident. Based on observation of the resident, there may be areas for improvement with regards to patient assessment and preparation, preparation of the physical location, clinical management plan, communication skills with patient/ next of kin, communication skills with healthcare team, technical skills, clinical judgment, organisation, and efficiency or professionalism. If there are areas for improvement, please select the appropriate choice and fill the comment section with specifics that will be useful for the resident. If there are no obvious areas for improvement, please select this choice before proceeding further.

For the entrustment-based discussion section for mini-CEX and EBD, the descriptors for the 9-point scale are different from the observation portion. Medical knowledge, clinical reasoning and where possible, practice-based learning and systems-based practice competencies are assessed based on milestone descriptors. It is compulsory for medical knowledge and clinical reasoning to be filled. A minimum of 2 entrustment questions should be asked and faculty must describe briefly what was discussed.

Faculty are expected to record the supervision level that the faculty would theoretically entrust the resident with for this particular EPA.

During the feedback, please comment on what the resident did well in and what can be improved upon, including resident’s readiness to handle variations of the situation or clinical case described above. Please also document the resident’s discussion with faculty and reflections on the case.

Faculty will be requested to indicate how receptive the resident was to feedback and if the resident demonstrated reflective learning via the dropdown box before submitting the form. A robust discussion and display of critical thinking skills should be encouraged.

## Entrustment Questions

Entrustment questions may come at the end of a direct observations assessment such as a mini-clinical examination (mini-CEX), direct observation of procedural skills (DOPS) or during an entrustment-based discussion (EBD) of a case that is co-managed by the faculty doing the assessment. They aim to allow the assessor to discern and be able to decide on an appropriate level of supervision for the resident based on clinical reasoning and knowledge, which may extend to systems-based practice or evidence-based practice.

It is important to note that these discussions need to follow an actual case.

The 4 main questions to ask and be answered by the end of the assessment are:

1. What did the learner do/ does the learner know what to do?
   1. This usually involves getting the learner to describe what he/she has done to demonstrate knowing the steps or process in managing the case.
2. Does the learner have adequate background knowledge?
   1. This usually involves getting the learner to describe why he/she does the various steps to demonstrate knowing the reasons for the various steps in managing this case. This should normally be done concurrently with demonstrating what to do.
3. Is the learner aware of the risks and possible complications of the activity i.e., what could go wrong? Is he/she able to display adaptability to these different scenarios?
   1. The assessor may ask "What are some of the common or known problems, risks or complications that can happen to this patient undergoing this procedure?"
   2. The assessor may ask “How will you handle the situation if you are managing this case by yourself?”
4. What would the learner do in case of unusual patients, rare findings, emerging risks, or complications?
   1. These are the “What ifs” type of questions. Example of “what ifs” questions are:
      1. If the patient has a different medical problem or physiological feature such as obesity, pregnancy, poorly controlled DM, recent URTI or COVID infection etc.
      2. If there is an unexpected surgical or laboratory findings
      3. If the case presents at night

*(In the source, sub-points are lettered a., b. and the “what ifs” examples numbered i., ii., iii.)*

A minimum of 2 entrustment questions should be asked for each WBA. Faculty must describe briefly what was discussed.

Please read the specifics and the discussion points for each assessment so that even if the suggested entrustment questions are not used, the entrustment questions asked by faculty will not overlap in scope with the other EBD or WBAs with entrustment questions for the EPA.

Examples of entrustment questions can be found under the respective EPA in the section titled Entrustment Questions for EPA x.

### Examples of Generic Entrustment questions for Evidence-based Medicine

(The bracketed numbers suggest questions relevant to the milestone scoring)

1. Where would you be able to locate evidence to guide you decision making for this patient? (1)
2. How would you integrate evidence-based medicine or guidelines with patient preference to aid you in the management of this patient? (5)
3. Do you know of any studies that could guide you in the management of this patient e.g., in blood management decisions? (3)

5. If current literature suggest opposite effects of an intervention, how would you decide what to do for this particular patient? (7)

### Examples of Generic entrustment questions for Systems based Practice

(The bracketed numbers suggest questions relevant to the milestone scoring)

1. How was care coordinated between the different disciplines for this patient? (7)
2. Who are the stakeholders and how can we ensure that all stakeholders are on the same page with regards to care of this patient? (3)
3. How would our anaesthetic plan affect the surgical plan or patient recovery? (5)
4. How can we ensure that the patient receives the best care without falling through cracks in the system? (7)
5. How do you think handover of care of the patient should be done? (7)
6. How would you ensure that the patient remains pain free after being discharged from the hospital back home? Is there any way that pain can be managed better in the community setting? (7)
7. What is value driven care and how is it applied to this patient? (7)

## Providing feedback

Feedback is an essential part of assessment. Without provision of constructive feedback, learning may not result from the assessment.

The principles of providing feedback include:

1. Timeliness. Provide feedback soon after the observation.
2. Focus on observed actions and behaviours rather than directed at the individual’s character, personality, or values.
3. Encourage self-assessment first e.g., “how do you think that went.”
4. Avoid facial expressions and body language that can give generalised feedback in a non-verbal way.
5. Be clear and specific.
6. Include positive feedback on things done well together with constructive feedback on things that can be done better to create a positive and supportive learning environment; positive feedback is important to reinforce desirable actions or behaviour.
7. Limit the feedback to the amount of information that the trainee can take in; focus on 2 or 3 points at most to work on.
8. Encourage reflection on your feedback by asking what all this means to the resident
9. Choose to use “I” rather than “you” in your feedback e.g., I wonder, I observed, I thought, as “you” can carry a judgemental connotation and potentially reduce acceptance of the feedback.
10. Practice mutual respect and listening to each other
11. Where feasible, develop a learning plan together or ask what the resident will do differently next time
12. Consider the use of feedback models.

### Feedback models

#### 1. DESC

- **D**escribe objectively what action or behaviour you observed which requires a change
- **E**xpress how you perceived the action affected others
- **S**uggest how to improve the situation
- Outline positive **C**onsequence (s) of improvement

#### 2. ADAPT

**A**sk

- Ask learner to assess own performance
- Assess learner’s insight
- Tailor feedback to learner perceptions
- Be an active listener: ask questions and reflect back

**D**iscuss

- Learner’s self-assessment
- Your observations about the performance: descriptive, specific, positive, corrective

**A**sk

- Ask what feedback means for the learner
- Ask about opportunities for development and change

**P**lan **T**ogether

- Ask for the action plan
- How can the learner get from point A to point B
- How can you help the learner

## Logbook

The training portfolio includes case records which encompass both case reflections and case log numbers. These will be reviewed at regular intervals by the program. For the EPAs that require case numbers and case reflections as part of the assessment, the minimum numbers will have to be fulfilled by the stipulated timeline before the level of entrustment is determined.

Further details on how to do a good case reflection and how to log case numbers will be provided in the training portfolio.
